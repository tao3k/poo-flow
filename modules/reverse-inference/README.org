#+TITLE: Reverse Inference

* Boundary

=ReverseInference= is a bounded candidate engine over source-labelled POO
claims. A caller declares claim objects, inference-step objects, hypothesis
objects, and one immutable Scheme GQL Query. The engine selects claim nodes
through the supported single-node GQL slice and uses Ascent to derive
source-linked steps, reachability, missing claims, and equality conflicts.

No domain incident, intrusion route, or policy decision is built into this
module. A result states that supplied claims support a candidate under the
declared steps. It does not authenticate a source, infer a historical
attribution, authorize an action, or assign a calibrated probability.

* Public values

[[file:objects.ss][objects.ss]] exports =PooFlowReverseInferenceCase.= and the
=poo-flow-inference-claim=, =poo-flow-inference-step=, and
=poo-flow-inference-hypothesis= constructors. An inference step has either
=presence= or =equal-value= join semantics. A hypothesis names its outcome,
candidate origin, and required claims. The first value is an observed
outcome; the second is an explanatory candidate.

[[file:funs.ss][funs.ss]] exports =poo-flow-reverse-inference-evaluate= and
=poo-flow-inference-hypothesis-result=. Each hypothesis result is one of
=supported=, =needs-evidence=, or =conflicted=. Missing claims identify
possible next evidence requests. Equality mismatches and conflicting values
are attached to the affected hypotheses; an unrelated hypothesis retains
its own status.

The Case is declarative. The private GQL and Ascent tuples are projections
of its POO values. The consumer must keep a source-authenticity and Host
admission boundary outside this engine.

* Qualification

Run =just test-file t/reverse-inference-core-test.ss=. The focused test checks
GQL selection, two competing hypotheses, a matching pair, a conflicting
source that cannot be hidden by a matching pair, and rejection of a negative
claim posed as positive evidence. A cyclic candidate graph reaches a finite
fixed point under Ascent's explicit input, derivation, and output bounds.
