#+title: RFC: Real-World AI Assurance Scenarios and Adapter Governance
#+startup: overview

* Status

- Architecture direction: proposed for discussion
- Scenario taxonomy: proposed for discussion
- Ownership boundaries: proposed for discussion
- Lean obligations: contributor qualification core locally discharged; scenario
  refinements remain open
- AXLE validation: required, not yet executed
- Cedar DRT and executable tests: required, not yet executed
- Runtime admission: not implemented by this RFC

This RFC extends the proof direction of
=45-114-cedar-dual-engine-policy-proof-boundary.org= into real enterprise and
personal AI infrastructure scenarios.

It does not replace =45-114=.  That RFC owns the general dual-engine policy
boundary.  This RFC owns the scenario catalog, threat models, scenario
projections, and the evidence required before a scenario can be called
assured.

* Problem

Agreement between two policy engines is useful, but engine agreement alone
does not prove that a real AI operation is safe.

A complete claim must also establish that:

1. the protected asset and requested action were represented correctly;
2. the principal, tenant, purpose, provenance, and capability context were
   neither omitted nor weakened;
3. the selected risk class requires the correct arbitration mode;
4. a disagreement, timeout, unavailable engine, stale policy, or incomplete
   input fails closed;
5. the final runtime action is the same action that was authorized;
6. the receipt preserves enough evidence for explanation, audit, recovery, and
   later verification.

The objective is therefore not merely “Cedar engine A equals Cedar engine B”.
The objective is an end-to-end assurance argument for concrete operations
performed by =wendao-episteme= and POO Flow.

* Architectural thesis

=wendao-episteme= and POO Flow together may form infrastructure for safe,
reliable, and trustworthy enterprise and personal AI, but they retain
different semantic ownership.

- =wendao-episteme= owns knowledge-source, claim, evidence, citation,
  provenance, and epistemic-governance semantics.
- =wendao-episteme= consumes the =poo-flow-core= parent contract and owns only
  its domain-specific governance projection and downstream embedding
  qualification; it does not become a parent owner of =poo-flow-core=.
- POO Flow owns POO-native module/profile composition, capability flow,
  agent/tool execution, runtime handoff, recovery, and operational receipts.
- =CedarAdapter= owns the canonical authorization projection and the
  dual-engine arbitration boundary.
- Cedar upstream owns Cedar language semantics, the definitional engine, the
  production engine, and DRT conformance assets.
- =gerbil-poo= remains the unique Scheme POO library and the physical object
  substrate used by POO Flow.

Neither a Git submodule nor an upstream checkout becomes a POO Flow runtime
owner merely because POO Flow pins or consumes it.

The concrete Wendao owner evidence remains:

- [[file:../../../packages/wendao-episteme/docs/rfcs/wendao-episteme-mix-profile-qualification-rfc.org][wendao-episteme Mix Profile qualification RFC]]

* Contributor ecosystem and engine boundary

=wendao-episteme= is the contributor repository for vertical-domain
construction, not a single vertical product.  Enterprises, individual users,
open-source maintainers, and domain specialists may jointly contribute and
maintain reusable modules, Profiles, Workflows, Knowledge, sources, claims,
evidence, citations, provenance, Policy, authorization, security, privacy,
compliance, audit, ownership, responsibility, accountability, and governance
projections.

This list is deliberately non-exhaustive.  Contributor assets inhabit an open,
multi-facet semantic space; they do not belong to one closed asset-kind
enumeration.  A single enterprise object may simultaneously be a Profile,
Policy projection, security control, Workflow, Knowledge projection, and audit
evidence carrier.  Domain owners may introduce new facet identities without
modifying the =poo-flow-core= engine.

Downstream users are free to select qualified contributor assets and rapidly
compose industry- or person-specific systems.  They obtain composition,
fixed-point evaluation, capability control, observability, recovery, and
receipt capabilities from the =poo-flow-core= engine rather than rebuilding
those facilities per industry.

This produces a deliberate two-level responsibility split:

1. the contributor ecosystem supplies and qualifies vertical-domain semantic
   objects;
2. =poo-flow-core= safely composes and executes those objects under explicit
   capability and policy evidence.

  => "Qualified"(a)
  and "QualifiedCapability"(a, x)
  and "UserAuthority"(u, x)
  and "Authorized"(p, a, x)
  and exists e; "AuthorityEvidence"(e, p, a, x) $

$ "ActionResponsibility"(a, x, r)
  => exists p, e;
    "Accountable"(p, a, r)
    and "ResponsibilityEvidence"(e, p, a, r) $
#+end_src

The intended theorem is parametric in the number of contributors, industries,
modules, Profiles, Workflows, and Knowledge objects.  Safety is derived from
qualification, stable identity, capability attenuation, and the admitted
=use-composition= result; it is not derived from trusting the repository as a
whole.

Authority, ownership, and responsibility remain distinct relations.  An owner
or maintainer is not implicitly authorized for every action.  Every governed
admission requires explicit authorization evidence, and every action
responsibility requires an accountable principal with responsibility
evidence.

* Assurance equation

  P_s and
  R_s and

$ "Elevated"(s) => "Mode"(s) = "StrictLockstep" $

$ "Mismatch"(s) or "IncompleteInput"(s) or "StalePolicy"(s)
  => "Decision"(s) = "Deny" $

The five conjuncts are deliberately independent:

- a Lean theorem about arbitration cannot compensate for an incomplete
  scenario projection;
- a DRT run cannot prove that a runtime executed the authorized action;
- two engines agreeing on a stale policy revision does not authorize the
  request;
- a runtime receipt without canonical input identity cannot be replayed as
  evidence of the same decision.

* One core, two governance profiles

Enterprise and personal AI must not fork policy semantics or create separate
engine implementations.  They compose different POO Profiles over the same
core objects and the same =CedarAdapter= contract.

#+begin_src scheme
(use-composition enterprise-ai
  (compose profiles
    base-profile
    wendao-episteme-mix
    cedar-policy-profile
    enterprise-governance-profile))

(use-composition personal-ai
  (compose profiles
    base-profile
    wendao-episteme-mix
    cedar-policy-profile
    personal-sovereignty-profile))
#+end_src

This remains the flat public =use-composition= surface.  This RFC introduces
neither =user-composition= nor a Cedar-specific composition macro.

The identifiers inside the example are scenario-level Profile object
placeholders, not newly authorized child-package public bindings.
=wendao-episteme-mix= preserves the already discussed Mix object role; its
eventual exported binding remains a decision for the Wendao owner RFC.

The two governance profiles may differ in policy data and admission choices:

- enterprise governance emphasizes tenant isolation, organizational roles,
  jurisdiction, retention, approval chains, and centralized audit;
- personal sovereignty emphasizes explicit consent, local-first execution,
  user-owned keys, revocation, deletion, provider choice, and intelligible
  receipts.

They must not differ in the meaning of authorization, canonical identity, or
fail-closed arbitration.

* Scenario contract

Every assurance scenario MUST define the following fields.

| Field | Required meaning |
|-------+------------------|
| Protected asset | The data, authority, money, identity, knowledge, or system state at risk |
| Principal | The human, organization, agent, service, or delegated subagent requesting authority |
| Action | The exact semantic operation, not only an API endpoint or tool name |
| Resource | The exact governed source, claim, model, secret, tool, tenant, policy, or runtime object |
| Context | Purpose, tenant, device, jurisdiction, time evidence, budget, approval, and other bounded observations |
| Entities | The complete Cedar entity closure needed by the policy |
| POO identity | The relevant immutable ProfileBundle, capability, module, and action identities |
| Risk class | Ordinary, elevated, or unknown; unknown is elevated |
| Arbitration mode | The mode selected from risk by trusted policy, never by the caller |
| Fail-closed events | Missing, stale, malformed, inconsistent, unavailable, timeout, or disagreement states |
| Receipt | Evidence sufficient to bind input, decision, engines, policy, and runtime handoff |
| Proof obligation | The Lean theorem and AXLE validation target |
| Executable obligation | DRT case and POO Flow integration/admission test |

* Scenario catalog

** S1: Knowledge-source admission and citation trust

=wendao-episteme= projects source identity, content identity, provenance,
publisher/author identity, acquisition method, freshness, evidence links, and
trust claims.  Cedar policy decides whether that source may enter a particular
knowledge scope or support a particular class of claim.

Threats include source substitution, citation laundering, stale evidence,
tenant-crossing source reuse, and treating “retrievable” as “trusted”.

- Elevated when the source can change policy, train/fine-tune a model, support
  a high-impact claim, or enter a shared enterprise knowledge base.
- Fail closed when provenance or content identity is incomplete, or when the
  two engines disagree.
- Receipt preserves source/content identity, provenance projection, target
  knowledge scope, policy revision, full Cedar outcomes, and admission result.

** S2: Retrieval and context assembly

Retrieval is an authorization operation, not only a relevance-ranking
operation.  The policy input binds tenant, user, purpose, source
classification, requested context window, and the exact evidence identities
selected for prompt assembly.

Threats include cross-tenant leakage, purpose expansion, hidden prompt
injection from untrusted evidence, and cached authorization reuse after a
source or policy changes.

- Cross-tenant, secret-bearing, or regulated context assembly is elevated.
- The final prompt/context digest must be derived from the authorized evidence
  set; post-authorization mutation invalidates admission.
- Receipt preserves both authorized evidence identities and assembled-context
  identity.

** S3: Tool invocation and external side effects

The governed action includes tool identity, operation, normalized arguments,
target resource, budget, side-effect class, and requested recovery behavior.

Threats include parameter smuggling, authorizing a read and executing a write,
tool binary replacement, budget escalation, and retrying a non-idempotent
effect under an old decision.

- Destructive, financial, external-message, publication, deployment, and
  credential-changing actions are elevated.
- Strict lockstep agreement does not itself execute the tool; runtime handoff
  must bind the authorized action identity to the executed effect.
- A retry requires a still-valid authorization or an explicitly proven retry
  capability.

** S4: Agent delegation and capability attenuation

A parent agent may delegate only a subset of its authority.  The child
principal, task identity, capability attenuation, resource scope, time/budget
limits, and further-delegation permission are explicit.

Threats include capability amplification, confused-deputy behavior, laundering
authority through a subagent, and reusing a delegation after revocation.

- Any authority expansion or further-delegation permission is elevated.
- The child’s effective capability set must be no greater than both the
  parent’s effective authority and the issued delegation.
- Receipt links parent request, delegation identity, child principal, narrowed
  capability, and every downstream action.

** S5: Secret and personal-data access

The policy distinguishes reading, transforming, exporting, retaining,
training on, and deleting sensitive data.  Provider destination, encryption
state, jurisdiction, user consent, and retention commitment belong to the
canonical input.

Threats include PII or secret exfiltration, provider substitution, silent
retention, secondary use, and using a model route not authorized for the data
class.

- Secret export, personal-data export, retention changes, and consent changes
  are elevated.
- Missing classification, consent, destination, or provider evidence fails
  closed.
- Personal AI receipts must remain intelligible and under the user’s control;
  enterprise receipts must also preserve tenant and organizational audit
  scope.

** S6: Model and provider routing

Model selection is governed by task class, data classification, provider,
model/executable identity, region, data-use terms, cost/budget, reliability
requirements, and local/offline constraints.

Threats include silent fallback to an unapproved provider, model alias drift,
data crossing a prohibited jurisdiction, and routing sensitive data to a model
with incompatible retention terms.

- Sensitive-data cloud routing and provider changes are elevated.
- A declared native/local route must not silently fall back to a legacy or
  remote process.
- Receipt binds the authorization input to the actual provider, model, region,
  and executable identity used.

** S7: Multi-tenant enterprise isolation

The canonical entity closure represents organization, tenant, project, user,
service account, delegated agent, resource ownership, and applicable role
relations.

Threats include incomplete entity closure, tenant alias confusion, shared-cache
poisoning, privilege inherited from the wrong organization, and audit evidence
visible across tenants.

- Cross-tenant access and tenant-administration changes are elevated.
- Tenant identity participates in authorization identity, cache identity,
  runtime resource scope, and receipt visibility.
- An incomplete or inconsistent entity closure fails closed before engine
  comparison.

** S8: Personal AI sovereignty and local-first operation

The user owns consent, provider preference, local-only constraints, personal
data scope, revocation, export, and deletion policy.  Offline operation may use
only explicitly admitted policy/evidence snapshots.

Threats include cloud fallback without consent, decisions from expired cached
policy, unavailable revocation evidence, and receipts that cannot be inspected
or removed by the user.

- Any transition from local to remote execution is elevated.
- Offline authorization is admitted only when policy, entity, capability, and
  revocation evidence freshness constraints all hold.
- Unavailability never turns a required remote check into implicit allow.

** S9: Policy, ProfileBundle, and knowledge-governance publication

Policy publication changes future authority.  ProfileBundle publication
changes executable composition.  Knowledge-governance publication changes
what sources or claims may be trusted.

Threats include self-authorization, rollback to a vulnerable revision,
publishing an unreviewed policy, split-brain active heads, and confusing source
revision with deployed revision.

- Publication, rollback, revocation, and active-head transitions are always
  elevated.
- The policy author, approver, publisher, and runtime activator may be distinct
  principals.
- Receipt binds source revision, proof/conformance evidence, published
  artifact, active head, and activation observation.

** S10: Disagreement, incident response, and continuable recovery

Dual-engine disagreement is an operational security event with first-class
evidence.  It is not collapsed into an ordinary Cedar deny and is never
silently resolved by choosing one engine.

Threats include hiding disagreement, retry storms, proceeding with a stale
allow, evidence loss during recovery, and human override without bounded
authority.

- The immediate decision is fail closed: deny or suspend before side effects.
- The runtime may offer a continuable recovery path only through an explicit
  recovery capability and a new, receipt-bearing decision.
- Quarantine preserves canonical input, both engine observations, policy and
  executable identities, runtime state, and the authorized human decision.

* Proof program

The proof program proceeds from scenario projection toward runtime refinement.
It must not start by inventing a universal theorem disconnected from an
executable scenario.

** Layer A: projection completeness

For each scenario, define the semantic input object and prove that every
security-relevant field participates in canonical identity or is explicitly
classified as receipt-only evidence.

#+begin_src typst
$ "SameInput"(x, y)
  => x."principal" = y."principal"
  and x."action" = y."action"
  and x."resource" = y."resource"
  and x."context" = y."context"
  and x."entities" = y."entities"
  and x."policyVersion" = y."policyVersion" $
#+end_src

** Layer B: risk projection

Prove that caller-controlled data cannot downgrade an elevated or unknown
operation to ordinary risk.

#+begin_src typst
$ "TrustedRisk"(s) in {"Elevated", "Unknown"}
  => "Mode"(s) = "StrictLockstep" $
#+end_src

** Layer C: dual-engine arbitration

Reuse and specialize the =45-114= arbitration theorems.  Comparison is over the
full comparable Cedar outcome, including determining and erroring policies,
not only allow/deny.

** Layer D: POO adapter refinement

Prove that the POO =CedarAdapter= object has exactly the admitted engine and
arbitration responsibilities and that scenario-specific data remains
invocation data rather than accidental mutable adapter slots.

** Layer E: runtime binding

Prove that the runtime effect, model route, context, publication, or delegation
executed is the exact semantic action authorized by the adapter.

#+begin_src typst
$ "Admitted"(a, r) and "Execute"(e, r)
  => "SemanticIdentity"(a) = "SemanticIdentity"(e) $
#+end_src

** Layer F: recovery monotonicity

Prove that disagreement, timeout, stale evidence, or incomplete input cannot
become allow merely because control resumes through a continuation.

* AXLE, Lean, DRT, and runtime roles

- Lean owns machine-checked semantic theorems and refinements.
- AXLE independently challenges the theorem statement, assumptions, missing
  cases, and correspondence claims; it does not replace local Lean builds.
- Cedar DRT compares executable behavior over generated and curated Cedar
  cases; it is finite conformance evidence, not universal proof.
- POO Flow tests bind canonical input, adapter outcome, ProfileBundle identity,
  and actual runtime behavior.
- Scenario receipts connect the four surfaces without claiming that any one of
  them subsumes the others.

Final-source AXLE validation must be reported separately from local Lean
success.  A policy block on external source validation remains a block and
must not be reported as green.

* Minimal assurance receipt

Every admitted or denied scenario result records:

- scenario identifier and semantic version;
- principal, action, resource, context, and entity-closure identities;
- Wendao source/claim/evidence identities when applicable;
- POO Flow ProfileBundle, capability, delegation, and action identities when
  applicable;
- Cedar policy-set identity and semantic version;
- definitional and production engine executable identities;
- requested and selected arbitration mode;
- complete comparable outcomes from all required engines;
- determining and erroring policy identities;
- risk-classification evidence;
- final admission, denial, suspension, or quarantine state;
- runtime handoff and observed execution identities, when execution occurs;
- Lean theorem-set identity, AXLE validation status, and DRT receipt identity;
- human approval or recovery-capability identity, when used.

Receipt redaction may hide protected values, but it must preserve stable
commitments sufficient to prove that the compared and executed objects were
the same objects.

* Admission rule

A scenario is not “safe”, “reliable”, “trusted”, or “assured” merely because:

- both engines returned allow;
- a Lean file compiled;
- DRT reported zero failures over a finite run;
- a ProfileBundle composed successfully;
- a human clicked approve;
- a receipt was emitted.

The assurance claim is admitted only when the scenario’s complete conjunction
is evidenced.

* Non-goals

- This RFC does not claim that all enterprise or personal AI risks reduce to
  Cedar authorization.
- It does not make Cedar a knowledge-reasoning engine.
- It does not move epistemic truth evaluation from =wendao-episteme= into POO
  Flow.
- It does not make POO Flow own Cedar upstream implementations or DRT.
- It does not create separate enterprise and personal engine forks.
- It does not treat finite differential testing as universal equivalence.
- It does not authorize runtime implementation before the scenario and
  ownership decisions are confirmed.

* Open decisions

1. Which S1 source-admission operations are ordinary, and which must always be
   elevated?
2. Is every cloud-provider transition elevated, or only transitions involving
   sensitive data or a changed provider trust domain?
3. Which personal-AI operations require an interactive human decision rather
   than a pre-authorized user Profile?
4. What is the minimum offline freshness and revocation evidence required for
   local operation?
5. Which receipt commitments must remain locally decryptable by a personal
   user, and which enterprise fields may be centrally escrowed?
6. Which first scenario should become the vertical proof/executable slice?

* Proposed first vertical slice

The proposed first slice is:

#+begin_quote
A =wendao-episteme= source is retrieved for a tenant-scoped AI context; the
source has explicit provenance and classification; POO Flow projects the exact
retrieval/context action; CedarAdapter performs strict lockstep authorization;
the assembled context identity is derived from the authorized evidence set;
and the runtime receipt proves that no different source, tenant, provider, or
context was used.
#+end_quote

This slice crosses epistemic trust, tenant isolation, policy authorization,
dual-engine arbitration, POO composition, runtime binding, and audit evidence
without requiring the entire AI infrastructure to be implemented first.
