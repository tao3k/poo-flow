* Decision

RFC 45-120-43 defines one authorization lifecycle contract.

For every non-committed recovery position, the exact owner-audit commitment
selects one authorization evidence object. Admission requires all of the
following facts at the same time:

1. the evidence is bound to the expected commitment and authority;
2. the complete production Cedar response equals the response produced by
   =Cedar.Spec.isAuthorized= for the recorded request, entities, and policies;
3. the response is =allow= and contains no erroring policy;
4. the policy snapshot epoch and fence equal the active recovery epoch and
   fence;
5. the credential is active at that epoch, has not been revoked, and belongs
   to the active generation;
6. the credential subject reaches the acting authority through the admitted
   authority path for the exact responsibility scope and commitment;
7. separation of duty holds between the acting authority and accountable
   identity for the exact commitment;
8. the decision, policy evidence, credential, accountability, responsibility,
   epoch, and fence agree with the commitment-authenticity evidence from RFC
   45-120-42.

This is the only semantic surface. No alternate admission path is defined.

** One canonical v1 admission predicate

This RFC defines one semantic contract for authorization admission:
=PooFlowProof.Enterprise.SourceBoundEffectCompletionRecoveryCedarAuthorizationLifecycleCore.SourceBoundEffectCompletionRecoveryCedarAuthorizationEvidence.Admitted=.
Every producer and consumer must prove that exact predicate over the complete
Cedar response, live credential, policy snapshot, authority path, separation
of duty, responsibility scope, accountability identity, runtime epoch, active
fence, and commitment identity.

#+begin_src typst
$ A_1(e) :=
  B_"commit"(e)
  and R_"exact"(e)
  and C_"live"(e)
  and F_"policy"(e)
  and H_"authority"(e)

$
#+end_src

There is no second admission relation. Evidence that does not satisfy
=$A_1$= is rejected; it is never reinterpreted, weakened, downgraded, or routed
through another predicate.

* Architectural boundary

The recovery engine consumes authorization evidence. It does not implement
Cedar evaluation, choose policies, issue credentials, resolve delegation, or
invent enterprise responsibility.

The owners are:

| Concern | Owner |
|---------+-------|
| Reference authorization decision | =Cedar.Spec.isAuthorized= |
| Production authorization decision | Cedar adapter or production Cedar engine |
| Policy snapshot identity and evidence root | Policy control plane |
| Credential lifecycle | Authentication and credential authority |
| Delegation path | Enterprise authorization graph |
| Separation of duty | Enterprise governance policy |
| Commitment and trace convergence | POO Flow recovery core |

The proof composes these independently owned facts without transferring their
ownership into the recovery engine.

* Lean-found architecture gaps

** Decision equality is too weak

Two engines may both return =allow= while naming different determining
policies or reporting different evaluation errors.

#+begin_src typst
$ "decision-only agreement"(r_R, r_P)
  "does not imply" "full-response agreement"(r_R, r_P) $
#+end_src

The independent theorem
=decisionOnlyAgreementDoesNotProveResponseAgreement= supplies a concrete
counterexample. Therefore the actual contract uses equality of the complete
=Cedar.Spec.Response=, not =Cedar.Spec.SameDecisions= and not a Boolean
allow/deny projection.

** An allow response with evaluation errors is not admissible

Full response equality alone could preserve an =allow= response whose
=erroringPolicies= set is nonempty. Enterprise recovery fails closed:

#+begin_src typst
$ "admitted"(e) "implies"
  "decision"("production response"(e)) = "allow"
  and "erroring policies"("production response"(e)) = emptyset $
#+end_src

The theorem =erroringCedarResponseRejectsAdmission= rejects this case.

** A verified signature does not prove a live credential

Cryptographic verification establishes a statement about a signature. It does
not establish that the signing credential is currently valid, unrevoked, or
in the active generation.

#+begin_src typst
$ "active"(c, t) :=
  "credential id"(c) != emptyset
  and "subject"(c) != emptyset
  and "valid from"(c) <= t
  and t < "valid until"(c)
  and (forall r in "revocations"(c): t < r)
  and "generation"(c) = "active generation"(c) $

$ "verified signature"(c) "does not imply" "active"(c, t) $
#+end_src

The independent theorem =verifiedSignatureDoesNotProveCredentialActive= gives
a revoked-credential counterexample. The actual theorem
=revokedCredentialRejectsAdmission= rejects a credential at or after its
revocation epoch.

** Policy identity without runtime freshness is insufficient

A valid response from an old policy snapshot cannot authorize a new recovery
attempt. Admission binds both the policy epoch and the active fence:

#+begin_src typst
$ "fresh policy"(e) :=
  "policy epoch"(e) = "runtime epoch"(e)
  and "snapshot fence"(e) = "active fence"(e) $
#+end_src

The theorem =stalePolicySnapshotRejectsAdmission= rejects a stale snapshot.

** Direct-identity equality destroys delegation

An earlier draft required:

#+begin_src typst
$ "credential subject" = "authority" $
#+end_src

That equation collapses every authorization into a direct authorization and
makes the delegation relation semantically unreachable. Lean exposed the
overconstraint when the authority-path obligation was modeled independently.

The canonical contract removes that equation. Both direct authority and
delegated authority are represented by the same relation:

#+begin_src typst
$ "authority path authorized"(
  "credential subject",
  "authority",
  "responsibility scope",
  "commitment"
) $
#+end_src

The relation owner proves whichever path is valid. POO Flow only requires the
proof for the exact scope and commitment.

** Authorization is not accountability

An allowed authority is not automatically the accountable identity. The
contract therefore keeps authorization, accountability, and responsibility
separate:

#+begin_src typst
$ "separation of duty satisfied"(
  "authority",
  "accountable identity",
  "commitment"
) $
#+end_src

This prevents the Cedar decision bit from erasing enterprise responsibility.

* Canonical admission predicate

Let =R= be the Cedar reference evaluator, =P= the production engine, =S= the
policy snapshot, =C= the credential, =A= the acting authority, =K= the exact
owner-audit commitment, and =t= the recovery epoch.

#+begin_src typst
$ "admitted"(e, A, K) :=
  "decision id"(e) != emptyset
  and "commitment"(e) = K
  and "authority"(e) = A
  and "accountability"(e) != emptyset
  and "responsibility scope"(e) != emptyset
  and "snapshot id"(e) != emptyset
  and "snapshot evidence root"(e) != emptyset
  and "policy epoch"(e) = "runtime epoch"(e)
  and "snapshot fence"(e) = "active fence"(e)
  and "production response"(e)
    = R("request"(e), "entities"(e), "policies"(e))
  and "decision"("production response"(e)) = "allow"
  and "erroring policies"("production response"(e)) = emptyset
  and "active"("credential"(e), "runtime epoch"(e))
  and "authority path authorized"(
    "credential subject"(e),
    "authority"(e),
    "responsibility scope"(e),
    "commitment"(e)
  )
  and "separation of duty satisfied"(
    "authority"(e),
    "accountability"(e),
    "commitment"(e)
  ) $
#+end_src

The registry is keyed by the exact commitment:

#+begin_src typst
$ "registry admits"(A, K) :=
  "admitted"("registry"(K), A, K) $
#+end_src

This makes authorization a property of the precise recovery action and its
evidence, rather than a reusable ambient =allow= bit.

credential status, delegation path, or governance fact.
