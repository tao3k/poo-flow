* Scope

This RFC defines coordinator admission after a terminal outcome.  It fixes:

- resumption admission;
- fresh execution identity;
- execution lineage;
- continuation-fence claiming;
- the distinction among resume, retry, replay, duplicate delivery, and
  finalization recovery;
- admission idempotency and crash ambiguity;
- budget and context behavior across lineage edges.

This RFC does not define:

- a new continuation implementation;
- a distributed continuation format;
- a second retry framework;
- a new persistence engine;
- a raw graph DSL;
- a public resumption macro.

* Central decision

No terminal outcome resumes itself.

All work after a terminal outcome begins with a coordinator decision over pure
POO-native values:

#+begin_example
PriorOutcomeReference
  × RecoveryProposal
  × FreshAdmissionContext
  -> RecoveryAdmissionDecision
#+end_example

An accepted decision produces a new admitted execution.  A rejected decision
produces a typed =ControlCondition= and terminal control evidence.

The prior outcome contributes evidence and lineage.  It contributes no ambient
authority.

* Identity model

The lineage model distinguishes:

- =DemandIdentity=: the semantic request;
- =DemandExecutionIdentity=: one admitted attempt;
- =LineageIdentity=: the immutable recovery lineage;
- =PriorOutcomeIdentity=: the terminal evidence being inspected;
- =RecoveryProposalIdentity=: the content identity of one proposal;
- =AdmissionIdentity=: one coordinator admission decision;
- =ContinuationFenceIdentity=: the process-local continuation boundary;
- =FenceClaimIdentity=: one atomic claim attempt.

No recovery operation reuses a prior =DemandExecutionIdentity=.

Duplicate delivery and finalization recovery are the only operations that may
refer to the same execution identity without creating a new one.  They do not
start a new evaluation.

* RecoveryProposal

=RecoveryProposal= is a native POO object.  It carries:

- the prior outcome reference;
- the requested recovery relation;
- a typed recovery input;
- principal and session evidence;
- explicit capability evidence;
- expected policy, evaluator, and observation versions;
- a fresh budget offer;
- a fresh admission-context reference;
- an idempotency identity.

It contains no callable continuation, mutable context, commit token, raw
predicate lambda, alist patch, or keyword-clause DSL.

The live continuation remains behind an opaque process-local runtime reference.

* Recovery relations

** Resume

=Resume= continues an eligible suspended computation.

Resume:

- requires a prior =ConditionOutcome= or =SuspendedOutcome=;
- requires a continuable condition, live continuation, or portable restart
  descriptor explicitly marked eligible;
- creates a new =DemandIdentity= when the typed recovery input changes the
  semantic request;
- always creates a new =DemandExecutionIdentity=;
- adds a =resumes= edge to the prior outcome;
- requires fresh policy, authority, context, fence, and budget admission.

Resume never inherits authority from the prior evaluator or handler.

** Retry

=Retry= evaluates the same semantic demand again after a retryable terminal
result.

Retry:

- preserves =DemandIdentity= only when semantic inputs are identical;
- always creates a new =DemandExecutionIdentity=;
- adds a =retries= edge to the prior execution and outcome;
- requires an explicit retryable condition and =RetryCapability=;
- requires fresh policy, context, and budget admission;
- cannot consume a continuation fence.

If semantic inputs change, the operation is a new demand related by
=supersedes=, not a retry.

** Replay

=Replay= re-evaluates recorded evidence in an isolated replay context.

Replay:

- may reuse the semantic =DemandIdentity=;
- always creates a new =DemandExecutionIdentity=;
- adds a =replays= edge to the recorded outcome;
- uses recorded observations or explicit replay inputs;
- cannot invoke a live continuation;
- cannot obtain admitted commit authority;
- cannot publish replay cache entries into an admitted cache;
- produces comparison and determinism evidence, not replacement history.

Replay never rewrites the outcome it examines.

** Duplicate delivery

A duplicate delivery presents the same admission or terminalization identity
again.

It:

- creates no new demand;
- creates no new execution;
- consumes no new budget;
- invokes no continuation;
- returns the already-bound admission, outcome, or receipt when content
  identity matches;
- fails closed when the repeated content conflicts.

Duplicate delivery is idempotency handling, not retry.

** Finalization recovery

Finalization recovery continues closure of an already-running execution after a
coordinator or process interruption.

It:

- retains the existing =DemandExecutionIdentity=;
- may complete missing cleanup, cache, reconciliation, or successor-context
  receipts;
- cannot restart evaluator computation under that identity;
- cannot invoke a continuation whose invocation status is uncertain;
- binds the same terminal outcome candidate or raises a typed conflict.

Finalization recovery is receipt-chain repair, not resume or retry.

* Eligibility matrix

| Prior evidence                                      | Resume | Retry | Replay | Duplicate or finalization recovery |
|-----------------------------------------------------+--------+-------+--------+------------------------------------|
| Completed =ResultOutcome=                           | no     | policy-defined | yes    | yes                                |
| Continuable =ConditionOutcome= with eligible handle | yes    | policy-defined | yes    | yes                                |
| =SuspendedOutcome= with live fence                   | yes    | policy-defined | yes    | yes                                |
| Portable descriptor without live continuation       | restart under resume admission | policy-defined | yes | yes |
| =DeniedOutcome=                                      | no     | only after fresh retry authorization | yes | yes |
| =CancelledOutcome=                                   | no     | only as a new authorized demand | yes | yes |
| Conflicting or incomplete receipt chain              | no     | no until typed closure decision | isolated diagnostic replay only | yes |

A portable restart does not pretend to invoke the original continuation.  It
constructs a fresh execution from the descriptor under the =resumes= lineage.

* Admission pipeline

The coordinator evaluates admission in this order:

1. validate content identities and prior terminal evidence;
2. classify the requested recovery relation;
3. validate outcome eligibility;
4. authenticate principal and session;
5. intersect capabilities with current policy;
6. validate evaluator, observation, and policy versions;
7. derive a narrowed fresh evaluation context;
8. admit a fresh budget reservation;
9. prepare a continuation-fence claim when live resume is requested;
10. bind the admission decision idempotently;
11. hand the admitted execution to the runtime boundary.

Failure at one step cannot be converted into success by a later step.

No step widens capability, redaction, retention, or observation scope.

* Continuation fence

A live continuation is referenced through an opaque, process-local fence.

The pure control plane constructs a =FenceClaimProposal=.  The runtime boundary
performs the atomic claim and returns a =FenceClaimReceipt=.

The claim verifies:

- process and runtime epoch;
- principal and session binding;
- policy and context binding;
- one-shot availability;
- expiry;
- expected prior outcome;
- admitted execution identity.

Only one claim may become effective for a one-shot continuation.

Concurrent losing claims return typed conflict or expiry evidence.  They do not
silently fall back to retry or portable restart.

* Fence lifecycle

#+begin_src mermaid
flowchart LR
  O["Eligible terminal outcome"] -->|"coordinator accepts a live-resume proposal"| P["Fence claim prepared"]
  P -->|"runtime identity and epoch still match"| C["Fence claimed"]
  P -->|"already claimed, expired, or mismatched"| R["Typed refusal"]
  C -->|"invocation begins under admitted execution identity"| I["Continuation invocation observed"]
  C -->|"claim revoked before invocation"| V["Fence revoked"]
  I -->|"one-shot use is recorded"| U["Fence consumed"]
  I -->|"invocation status becomes uncertain"| Q["Invocation uncertainty"]
  Q -->|"same execution closes without reinvocation"| F["Typed terminal closure"]
  R -->|"fresh policy may consider another proposal"| N["New recovery proposal"]
  V -->|"fresh admission may use portable restart"| N
  U -->|"resumed execution reaches exactly one outcome"| T["New terminal outcome"]
#+end_src

There is no edge from invocation uncertainty back to continuation invocation.

* Crash and uncertainty rule

The architecture does not claim physical exactly-once continuation invocation.

If a process fails after a fence is claimed and before durable invocation
evidence establishes whether invocation occurred:

- the coordinator does not invoke the continuation again;
- the same execution is terminalized with typed
  =ContinuationInvocationUncertain= evidence;
- any portable restart requires a new recovery proposal and fresh admission;
- the new execution links to the uncertain outcome;
- prior cost remains accounted.

This converts ambiguity into explicit evidence instead of hidden duplicate
effects.

* Admission idempotency

An admission idempotency identity covers:

- recovery proposal identity;
- prior outcome identity;
- principal and session;
- expected versions;
- fresh context identity;
- budget offer;
- requested recovery relation.

An identical duplicate returns the existing =RecoveryAdmissionDecision=.

A conflicting duplicate under the same idempotency identity fails closed.  It
cannot allocate a second budget reservation or prepare a second fence claim.

Admission idempotency does not make execution, continuation invocation, or
external effects physically exactly once.

* Context and authority

Every admitted recovery execution receives a fresh immutable context.

The context is derived by narrowing:

#+begin_example
fresh base context
  ∩ current principal authority
  ∩ current policy
  ∩ recovery capability
  ∩ prior outcome observation boundary
#+end_example

The prior context may supply content-identified evidence.  It is not copied as
live authority.

Redaction and retention may only remain equal or become stricter.

* Budget behavior

Each new resume, retry, or replay execution receives a fresh reservation.

The lineage report distinguishes:

- prior historical cost;
- new admission reservation;
- new execution consumption;
- unused return;
- cumulative lineage cost.

Historical cost is not charged a second time.  It also cannot be erased.

Duplicate delivery consumes no new evaluation budget.  Finalization recovery
may consume an explicitly bounded closure budget, recorded under the existing
execution identity.

* Lineage graph

The lineage is an append-only typed POO relation graph.

#+begin_src mermaid
flowchart TD
  D0["Demand A / Execution 1"] -->|"completes with suspension evidence"| O0["Suspended outcome"]
  O0 -->|"resumes with typed recovery input and fresh admission"| D1["Demand B / Execution 2"]
  D1 -->|"fails with explicitly retryable condition"| O1["Typed condition outcome"]
  O1 -->|"retries the same semantic demand under new budget"| D2["Demand B / Execution 3"]
  O0 -->|"replays recorded evidence in isolated context"| D3["Demand A / Replay execution 4"]
  O1 -.->|"duplicate delivery returns the bound receipt"| O1
  D2 -->|"coordinator interruption leaves closure incomplete"| P["Pending finalization receipts"]
  P -->|"same execution identity closes reconciliation only"| O2["Terminal outcome for Execution 3"]
#+end_src

Typed lineage edges are:

- =resumes=;
- =retries=;
- =replays=;
- =supersedes=;
- =duplicate-of=;
- =recovers-finalization-of=.

=resumes=, =retries=, =replays=, and =supersedes= create a new execution node.
=duplicate-of= and =recovers-finalization-of= do not.

Lineage edges cannot mutate or replace their source node.

* Control-condition behavior

Admission failure produces a typed =ControlCondition= with one terminal control
decision:

- =Suspend= when later fresh evidence may make admission possible;
- =Deny= when current policy or authority refuses the operation;
- =Cancel= when an authorized cancellation closes the proposal.

=Continue= authorizes creation of =AdmittedRecovery=.  It is not returned as a
standalone successful recovery value.

An admission handler cannot return =Resume=.

* Publication and cache boundary

Resume, retry, and replay start with distinct cache namespaces.

- resume may read qualified lineage evidence but does not inherit unpublished
  cache state;
- retry may reuse only cache entries qualified for its fresh versions and
  context;
- replay uses an isolated replay namespace;
- duplicate delivery returns an existing receipt without cache publication;
- finalization recovery may finish an already-qualified publication but cannot
  qualify new evaluator results.

No lineage relation promotes simulation or replay cache entries into an
admitted cache.

* Public interface boundary

Ordinary user composition does not expose admission receipts, fence claims, or
lineage construction.

Advanced extensions use native =gerbil-poo= objects and prototype operations
for:

- recovery proposal specialization;
- admission-policy specialization;
- typed lineage relations;
- fence and recovery evidence inspection;
- recovery explanation and simulation.

No public macro is introduced by this RFC.

* Failure behavior

The coordinator fails closed on:

- resume from an ineligible outcome;
- retry without explicit retry authorization;
- replay with live continuation or commit authority;
- reuse of a prior execution identity for new evaluation;
- continuation fence mismatch, expiry, or double claim;
- reinvocation after uncertain invocation;
- conflicting admission idempotency content;
- semantic input change mislabeled as retry;
- portable restart represented as live continuation invocation;
- authority, redaction, retention, or observation widening;
- new budget consumption hidden as duplicate delivery;
- evaluator restart disguised as finalization recovery.
