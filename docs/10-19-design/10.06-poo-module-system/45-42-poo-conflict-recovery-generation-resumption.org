#+title: POO Conflict Recovery, Generation Transition, and Bounded Resumption
#+subtitle: Pure conflict evidence, live recovery authority, and continuable Scheme execution
#+startup: overview

* RFC status

- Status :: Accepted design
- Kind :: Normative design module
- Semantic core :: =gerbil-poo= objects and POO Flow evaluation
- Related module :: =45-41-poo-profile-slot-contribution-priority.org=

This module defines how POO Flow reports and recovers from semantic composition conflicts. It uses the advantages of Scheme first-class continuations and continuable conditions without placing a live continuation, capability, or mutable handle inside a pure POO evidence object.

* Core decision

#+begin_quote
Conflict evidence is a pure and replayable POO object. A continuation or resumption is a capability-scoped live runtime resource. Every recovery decision that changes composition semantics derives a new composition generation.
#+end_quote

A handler must not mutate a conflicted generation in place. Resuming a suspended caller does not authorize the old generation to pretend that it produced a result under new semantics.

* Three-part recovery model

** Pure conflict evidence

When equal-priority contributions cannot be combined canonically, generation =G= seals a pure conflict-evidence object containing at least:

- the semantic slot identity;
- the composition generation identity;
- candidate identities, exact priorities, and provenance;
- the canonical native operation that was considered;
- why no canonical combination was admitted;
- the affected demand root and current-generation SCC;
- the classes of recovery that may be requested.

Pure conflict evidence:

- is canonicalizable, cacheable, and replayable;
- contains no continuation, resumption, capability, authority, lease, or mutable handle;
- does not force dormant business values merely to produce an explanation;
- remains valid as historical evidence after a later generation recovers.

** Live recovery portal

The runtime may raise the conflict as a continuable condition and expose a live POO recovery portal in the current lexical or dynamic evaluation scope.

The portal:

- is not stored in a global registry;
- is not serializable, cacheable, or part of pure conflict evidence;
- is capability-scoped and bound to one conflict and generation;
- is one-shot by default;
- carries a finite recovery budget;
- accepts only an ordinary POO =RecoveryDecision= value;
- expires when its evaluation scope closes or its authority is consumed.

An implementation may use first-class continuations, =call/cc=, or a continuable exception mechanism internally. The public protocol does not expose a raw continuation as the ordinary interface.

** Recovery decision

An authorized handler submits a POO =RecoveryDecision=. A decision may request:

- a different priority for a contribution;
- replacement or removal of a contribution;
- an explicitly constructed Profile operand;
- an authorized canonical resolver POO object;
- explicit termination of the evaluation.

The decision is data with POO identity and provenance. It is not an arbitrary callback that mutates evaluator state.

* Generation transition

A semantic decision follows this transition:

#+begin_src text
generation G conflict
-> RecoveryDecision
-> validate authority, scope, and budget
-> derive generation G+1
-> reevaluate the affected slot and downstream SCCs
-> resume the suspended demand against G+1
#+end_src

Generation =G= remains a sealed historical result containing its conflict. Generation =G+1= has a new semantic identity derived from the admitted decision and its changed composition inputs.

#+begin_src mermaid
flowchart LR
    G["Generation G evaluates a demanded slot"] -->|"equal-priority values have no canonical combination"| C["Seal pure conflict evidence"]
    C -->|"raise a continuable condition in lexical scope"| P["Live recovery portal"]
    P -->|"no authorized handler is available"| F["Fail closed with conflict evidence"]
    P -->|"handler submits a POO RecoveryDecision"| V["Validate capability, scope, and budget"]
    V -->|"decision is invalid or unauthorized"| F
    V -->|"decision is admitted"| N["Derive generation G+1"]
    N -->|"record the causal transition from G"| E["Recovery decision receipt"]
    N -->|"reevaluate affected slot and downstream SCCs"| R["Resume suspended demand against G+1"]
    R -->|"another conflict consumes recovery budget"| P
    R -->|"a canonical result is obtained"| K["Commit the G+1 result"]
#+end_src

* Retry and semantic recovery

Retry and semantic recovery are different:

- Operational retry :: Repeats a transient operation without changing semantic inputs and may remain in the same semantic generation.
- Semantic recovery :: Changes priority, a resolver, a Profile operand, admission, or another composition input and must derive a new generation.
- Caller resumption :: Continues the suspended consumer after reevaluation; it does not rewrite the result of the old generation.

A retry that changes semantic identity is misclassified and must be promoted to semantic recovery.

* Bounded continuation use

A raw multi-shot continuation is not the public recovery interface. Repeated invocation could duplicate runtime effects, commits, capability consumption, or Marlin handoff.

The default portal is therefore one-shot:

1. A handler submits one decision.
2. The runtime consumes the portal authority.
3. An admitted semantic decision derives one new generation.
4. A new conflict may create a new portal with a reduced root-demand budget.

Reusable recovery behavior is expressed by a pure POO policy that can produce a new decision for a new portal. It is not expressed by replaying an old live continuation.

* Recovery budget and loop detection

Every root demand carries a finite recovery budget. The budget may bound:

- semantic generation transitions;
- cumulative reevaluation cost;
- conflict count;
- repeated equivalent decisions;
- elapsed operational time where the runtime policy permits it.

Submitting a decision with the same semantic identity against the same conflict must be recognized as a recovery loop. Budget exhaustion fails closed and seals evidence for the complete causal chain.

#+begin_src mermaid
flowchart LR
    D["Admitted RecoveryDecision"] -->|"changes semantic composition identity"| N["New generation"]
    N -->|"encounters another conflict"| B["Consume root-demand recovery budget"]
    B -->|"budget remains and decision identity differs"| D
    B -->|"equivalent decision repeats"| L["Recovery-loop evidence"]
    B -->|"budget is exhausted"| X["Bounded fail-closed result"]
    L -->|"prevent continuation replay"| X
#+end_src

* Authority and capability boundaries

Recovery authority is narrower than evaluation authority:

- observing conflict evidence does not grant recovery authority;
- possessing a priority value does not grant authority to replace a contribution;
- a resolver is admitted only for its declared semantic slots and capability scope;
- a handler cannot transfer a portal outside its live scope;
- a recovery decision cannot enlarge its own capability;
- =force= priority cannot bypass recovery authorization.

The runtime validates authority before deriving a new generation. An unauthorized decision becomes rejection evidence and does not mutate generation =G=.

* Recovery decision receipt

Every admitted semantic recovery produces a pure receipt that links:

- the generation =G= conflict evidence identity;
- the recovery portal scope identity without serializing the portal itself;
- the handler and capability evidence permitted for disclosure;
- the =RecoveryDecision= identity and canonical projection;
- the generation =G+1= identity;
- the slots and SCCs selected for reevaluation;
- consumed and remaining recovery budget;
- the final commit, subsequent conflict, or fail-closed result.

Retention and redaction follow the active observability Profile. Redaction must not remove the causal link required to prove why =G+1= exists.

* Failure behavior

The runtime fails closed when:

- no authorized handler is present;
- the portal has expired or was already consumed;
- the decision is outside the portal scope;
- capability or authority validation fails;
- the decision does not change the conflicting semantic identity;
- a recovery loop is detected;
- the recovery budget is exhausted;
- generation =G+1= cannot establish canonical composition.

Failure preserves pure evidence and never silently selects a contribution by operand order.

* Public-interface constraint

Ordinary users continue to construct composition through Profiles:

#+begin_src scheme
(user-composition my-system
  (compose profiles
    base
    wendao-episteme
    production))
#+end_src

Recovery does not add conditionals, recovery clauses, or a new macro language to =user-composition=. Advanced recovery is expressed through POO policy, resolver, and decision objects supplied through existing Profile composition.

Concrete continuation and condition primitives must be selected only after verification against the real Gerbil runtime. This RFC defines their semantic boundary, not an invented wrapper API.

* Implementation checklist

- [ ] Define canonical pure POO conflict evidence.
- [ ] Prove that conflict evidence contains no live continuation, capability, lease, or mutable handle.
- [ ] Define a lexical or dynamic live recovery portal with one-shot default authority.
- [ ] Define =RecoveryDecision= as an ordinary POO object family.
- [ ] Validate decision capability, authority scope, and semantic effect before recovery.
- [ ] Derive a new generation for every semantics-changing decision.
- [ ] Preserve generation =G= as sealed historical conflict evidence.
- [ ] Resume suspended callers only against the derived generation.
- [ ] Separate operational retry receipts from semantic recovery receipts.
- [ ] Add finite root-demand recovery budgets.
- [ ] Detect repeated equivalent recovery decisions.
- [ ] Prevent portal serialization, caching, global registration, and out-of-scope transfer.
- [ ] Generate a pure recovery decision receipt linking =G= to =G+1=.
- [ ] Verify that redaction retains the mandatory causal transition.
- [ ] Validate implementation choices against real Gerbil continuation and condition primitives.

* Non-goals

- Do not expose raw =call/cc= as the ordinary public recovery API.
- Do not store live resumptions inside pure POO objects.
- Do not mutate a sealed generation in place.
- Do not treat semantic recovery as operational retry.
- Do not add a recovery DSL or domain-specific public macro.
- Do not create a global continuation or recovery registry.
- Do not silently resolve a conflict by presentation order.
