#+title: Explicit Temporal Observations, Clock Domains, and Replay
#+startup: overview

* Status

This RFC records the accepted temporal semantics for POO Flow evaluation,
admission, leases, deadlines, freshness, and replay.

It extends resource admission, distributed evaluation, compatibility evidence,
and lifecycle policy. It forbids hidden live-clock reads inside pure POO
fixed-point evaluation.

* Typst formalization: explicit temporal observations

Pure evaluation receives time as an immutable observation with an explicit
clock domain, provenance identity, and observation identity.  Missing time
suspends a time-dependent decision; it never authorizes an implicit live clock
read.

#+begin_src typst
#let time-observation(domain, instant, provenance, identity) = (
  domain: domain,
  instant: instant,
  provenance: provenance,
  identity: identity,
  immutable: true,
)

$ pure-temporal-evaluation(e)
  ==> explicit(e.time-observation)
  and immutable(e.time-observation)
  and not reads-live-clock(e) $

$ missing(time-observation)
  ==> suspend(temporal-decision)
  and not guess(current-time) $
#+end_src

Clock roles are not interchangeable.  Elapsed duration uses a monotonic domain;
logical/version time is ordering evidence; authoritative service time requires
trusted provenance.  Cross-domain comparison requires explicit conversion
evidence.

#+begin_src typst
#let comparable(a, b, evidence) = (
  a.domain == b.domain
  or valid-clock-conversion(evidence, a.domain, b.domain)
)

$ elapsed-duration(t_0, t_1)
  ==> t_0.domain = "monotonic"
  and t_1.domain = "monotonic" $

$ a.domain != b.domain and missing(conversion-evidence)
  ==> not comparable(a, b, none) $

$ replay(input, observation, policy)
  = evaluate(input, observation, policy) $

$ observation-a != observation-b
  ==> evaluation-identity(input, observation-a, policy)
  != evaluation-identity(input, observation-b, policy) $

$ temporal-proposal(p)
  ==> not terminate-authority(p)
  and not fence-authority(p)
  and not reclamation-authority(p) $
#+end_src

The Lean anchor is =PooFlowProof.PooC3.ExplicitTemporalObservations=.  It

the runtime authority that admits, fences, suspends, or releases resources.

* Central decision

A pure POO fixed point may receive an immutable =TimeObservation= as an
explicit input.

It must not call a wall clock, monotonic clock, network time service, or other
live time source implicitly during pure evaluation.

The same semantic and observation inputs must produce the same pure result.

* POO semantic ownership

Temporal policy is represented by ordinary higher-order POO objects. Native
prototype composition defines:

- freshness contracts;
- deadline policy;
- lease timing policy;
- reversion windows;
- continuation expiry policy;
- retention windows;
- uncertainty admission;
- clock-source trust.

The public extension interface remains POO-native. Clock role, expiry behavior,
and uncertainty handling must not become an unstructured macro token DSL.

Pure policy may produce temporal evidence or proposals. It has no authority to
terminate a flow, fence a worker, expire a continuation, reclaim a resource, or
close a reversion window.

* Clock roles

At least four clock roles remain distinct.

** Wall-clock time

Wall-clock time supports:

- human-readable audit timestamps;
- calendar policy;
- external certificate validity;
- cross-system business time.

It may jump because of synchronization, administrative change, leap handling,
virtual-machine restoration, or provider correction.

Wall-clock differences must not measure local execution duration.

** Monotonic time

Monotonic time supports:

- local evaluation deadlines;
- elapsed duration;
- scheduler waiting time;
- local lease-renewal margin;
- fairness and starvation observations.

A monotonic value is meaningful only within its identified clock domain.
Monotonic timestamps from different hosts or processes must not be compared as
if they shared an origin.

** Logical and version time

Logical and version time supports:

- active-head versions;
- fencing tokens;
- compare-and-swap resource versions;
- event ordering;
- generation transition history.

Distributed safety ordering prefers authoritative logical versions and fencing
over assumptions of perfectly synchronized wall clocks.

** Authoritative service time

Some leases, certificates, governance windows, or external resources use time
owned by a specific authority.

A client-local clock may wake a check early. It does not replace an
authoritative observation from that service.

* Time observation

An immutable =TimeObservation= records:

- clock source identity;
- clock domain identity;
- wall, monotonic, logical, or authoritative role;
- observed instant or counter;
- uncertainty interval;
- precision;
- acquisition receipt;
- trust or attestation identity;
- observation freshness;
- evaluation attempt identity;
- observation schema identity.

The observation identity covers all fields that affect temporal evaluation.

Clock synchronization status alone is not proof that uncertainty is zero.

* Uncertainty intervals

Temporal policy evaluates intervals rather than pretending every timestamp is
exact.

For a current-time interval =[earliest, latest]= and a decision boundary:

- definitely valid means the complete interval satisfies the validity rule;
- definitely expired means the complete interval satisfies the expiry rule;
- an interval crossing the boundary produces temporal suspension.

Policy must not choose whichever endpoint permits the desired action.

Different boundaries may use different conservative proof rules, but every
rule must expose its uncertainty assumptions in evidence.

* Lease time and fencing

A lease authority records:

- lease identity;
- holder identity;
- lease version;
- expiry in the authority clock domain;
- fencing token;
- renewal and release receipts.

Renewal uses versioned compare-and-swap.

A holder approaching an uncertain expiry boundary stops producing new
authority-bearing results according to policy.

The coordinator uses an authoritative lease observation to determine current
ownership.

Fencing closes the stale-holder race even when a stale holder's local clock
still indicates validity.

Local clock belief must not override a newer fencing token.

* Distributed deadlines

A coordinator monotonic timestamp must not be sent to another host and treated
as directly comparable local time.

Distributed deadline delegation uses:

- admitted duration budget;
- coordinator attempt identity;
- lease and fencing identities;
- worker-local start observation;
- worker-local elapsed-time evidence;
- budget-consumption receipt;
- coordinator result-admission evidence.

The worker may enforce its allocation using its local monotonic domain.
The coordinator decides whether the result remains admissible under the
current attempt and fence.

Retry and worker migration do not reset the parent duration or resource budget.

* Freshness and cache reuse

The following identities remain separate:

- freshness duration and rule belong to temporal policy identity;
- the current =TimeObservation= belongs to proof and admission evidence;
- a pure semantic value does not acquire a new identity merely because time
  advanced;
- a freshness proof receives a new evidence identity when its time observation
  changes.

Every use of a cached proof readmits freshness with a current admitted
observation.

A timer or watcher wake-up is a hint. The awakened evaluator obtains a fresh
authoritative time observation before making a decision.

* Temporal lifecycle proposals

Crossing a temporal boundary permits pure policy to produce proposals such as:

- =ReversionWindowClosedProposal=;
- =ContinuationExpiryProposal=;
- =RetentionLeaseExpiryProposal=;
- =DeadlineTerminationProposal=.

The proposal is not the live action.

Fencing, termination, reclamation, isolation, and destructive contract still
require:

- current resource observation;
- exact live capability;
- version and fencing checks;
- action receipt;
- unknown-outcome reconciliation.

Time passage alone does not perform destructive work.

* Deterministic replay

Historical replay uses the recorded =TimeObservation=, uncertainty interval,
and logical ordering evidence.

Replay must not read the current live clock to reproduce a historical temporal
decision.

This allows replay to explain why the original evaluation produced proof,
violation, or suspension.

Asking whether an item is valid now is a new live admission with a new time
observation. It is not historical replay.

Replayed evidence does not reacquire live authority.

* Continuable temporal conditions

Clock unavailability, excessive skew, and boundary-crossing uncertainty may
raise continuable conditions with constrained restart roles:

- acquire a more authoritative time observation;
- wait for uncertainty to narrow;
- use another clock authority admitted by policy;
- suspend the decision;
- cancel the attempt.

Changing the clock observation while preserving the same temporal policy may
continue evaluation with a new evidence cut.

Changing the policy, clock semantics, deadline, or window creates a new
proposal identity. A restart must not rewrite the prior proof in place.

* Cross-host clock skew

Cross-host policy records an uncertainty bound or authority relationship rather
than assuming synchronized equality.

When no admitted bound exists:

- wall-clock ordering across hosts remains unknown;
- monotonic timestamps remain incomparable;
- logical version and fencing evidence determine safety ordering;
- temporal admission requiring stronger ordering suspends or fails closed.

NTP status, clock offset estimates, and provider attestations are evidence
inputs. None automatically proves a zero-skew global clock.

* Failure taxonomy

The implementation must distinguish at least:

- hidden live-clock read attempted;
- clock source unavailable;
- clock domain identity missing;
- cross-domain monotonic comparison attempted;
- time observation stale;
- uncertainty interval too wide;
- temporal boundary unresolved;
- lease renewal compare-and-swap conflict;
- stale fencing token;
- distributed deadline budget exhausted;
- cached proof freshness rejected;
- timer wake without authoritative observation;
- replay attempted with live time;
- clock authority rejected;
- temporal action capability missing;
- temporal receipt incomplete.

* Required invariants

1. Pure fixed-point evaluation performs no hidden live-clock reads.
2. Every temporal decision receives an explicit immutable observation.
3. Wall, monotonic, logical, and authoritative time remain distinct.
4. Monotonic timestamps are compared only within one clock domain.
5. Uncertainty is represented and evaluated explicitly.
6. Boundary-crossing uncertainty does not produce proof or violation.
7. Fencing, not local clock belief, closes stale lease ownership.
8. Distributed deadlines preserve parent budget across hosts and retries.
9. Cached proof freshness is readmitted with current evidence.
10. Time passage produces proposals, not destructive live actions.
11. Historical replay uses recorded observations, not current time.
12. Continuable restarts do not rewrite temporal semantics in place.
13. Cross-host skew assumptions remain explicit evidence.
14. Public temporal policy extension remains POO-native.
