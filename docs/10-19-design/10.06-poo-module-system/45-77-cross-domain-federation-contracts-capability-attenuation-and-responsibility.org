#+title: Cross-Domain Federation Contracts, Capability Attenuation, and Responsibility
#+startup: overview

* Status

This RFC records the accepted cross-organization federation semantics for POO
module and submodule composition.

It extends scoped trust anchors, governance, graph-wide compatibility,
capabilities, evidence import, and multi-scope publication.

* Typst formalization: bounded federation without a global root

Federation creates an explicit relation between independently owned domains; it
does not create a global root authority.  An agreement is admitted only when
requirement, offer, trust, disclosure, evidence, capability constraints,
responsibility, and temporal checks all hold.

#+begin_src typst
#let federation-checks(requirement, offer, current) = (
  requirement.contracts == offer.contracts
  and accepted-trust-anchors(requirement, offer)
  and disclosure-allowed(offer.projection, requirement.policy)
  and evidence-requirements-met(requirement, offer)
  and capability-constraints-met(requirement, offer)
  and responsibility-allocated(requirement, offer)
  and temporal-requirements-hold(current.time-observation)
)

$ federation(agreement)
  ==> federation-checks(agreement.requirement, agreement.offer, current)
  and not global-root-authority(agreement) $

$ trust(domain-a, domain-b)
  and trust(domain-b, domain-c)
  ==> not implicit-trust(domain-a, domain-c) $

$ remote-candidate(candidate)
  ==> local-admission-required(candidate) $
#+end_src

Delegation is monotone attenuation: scope and action cannot expand, expiry
cannot extend, and delegation depth cannot increase.  Agreements expose only
the admitted evidence projection; remote internal graphs and capabilities are
not imported implicitly.

#+begin_src typst
$ attenuate(source, delegated)
  ==> delegated.scope subset.eq source.scope
  and delegated.actions subset.eq source.actions
  and delegated.expiry <= source.expiry
  and delegated.delegation-depth <= source.delegation-depth $

$ federation-projection(projection)
  ==> not imports-remote-internal-graph(projection)
  and not imports-remote-capabilities(projection) $

$ revoked(agreement) or expired(agreement)
  ==> not continued-local-use(agreement) $

$ transfer-ownership(domain)
  ==> exact-transfer-contract(domain)
  and valid-transfer-authority(domain) $
#+end_src

* Central decision

Federation does not place participating organizations under one global root
authority.

Every domain retains ownership of its identities, policies, resources, module
revisions, evidence, active heads, capability issuance, and governance.

Interoperation uses explicit POO federation relations with bounded contracts,
evidence, disclosure, and authority.

Trust is not transitive by default.

* Domain ownership

Every participating domain owns:

- =DomainIdentity=;
- trust anchors;
- principals and role policy;
- module and submodule revisions;
- resources and active heads;
- capability issuers;
- evidence retention and disclosure policy;
- revocation authority;
- incident and governance responsibility.

A federation agreement does not transfer ownership unless an exact ownership
transfer contract and authority explicitly prove that change.

Remote internal POO graphs and capabilities are not imported implicitly.

* POO semantic ownership

Federation requirement, offer, compatibility, agreement, attenuation, and
responsibility policy are ordinary higher-order POO objects.

Native prototype composition defines:

- exported and imported contract projections;
- trust and evidence requirements;
- data and effect scopes;
- capability delegation;
- confidentiality and residency;
- failure and revocation policy;
- governance and responsibility.

The public federation interface remains POO-native. Agreement modes,
delegation, trust, and degraded behavior must not become unstructured macro
tokens or a hidden global registry.

* Federation requirement

A local =FederationRequirement= declares:

- required contract identities;
- accepted evidence roles;
- freshness and verification requirements;
- admitted data and effect scopes;
- capability attenuation rules;
- residency and confidentiality requirements;
- failure and revocation behavior;
- local governance requirements.

The requirement is owned by the importing domain.

* Federation offer

A remote =FederationOffer= declares:

- provided contract identities;
- exported projections;
- evidence-producing authorities;
- supported protocol and schema identities;
- owned resources and actions;
- retained rights and restrictions;
- provider governance and revocation policy.

The offer exports a scoped projection. It does not disclose the complete remote
definition graph by default.

* Federation agreement proposal

A pure compatibility closure evaluates one exact requirement and offer pair.

It produces a =FederationAgreementProposal= only when all directional
obligations are proven.

The evaluator must not silently negotiate to a lowest common denominator.
Missing compatibility, disclosure, trust, residency, or authority remains a
federation suspension or violation.

* Agreement identity and scope

A federation agreement binds:

- participating domain identities;
- agreement revision;
- imported and exported contract identities;
- module and submodule scope paths;
- trust anchors and verification policy;
- schema and protocol compatibility;
- data disclosure and residency;
- action and effect responsibility;
- capability delegation depth;
- freshness, expiry, and renewal;
- revocation propagation;
- incident notification;
- retention and erasure obligations;
- failure and degraded-operation policy;
- governance quorum;
- dispute and audit evidence.

A new agreement revision creates a new admission boundary.

Old agreement evidence remains historical. It does not authorize new operations
under the new revision.

* Federated module identity

A cross-domain module instance identity includes:

#+begin_example
local composition root
+ federation agreement revision
+ remote domain identity
+ remote exported module anchor and revision
+ local import scope path
+ contract projection identity
#+end_example

A package or repository name is not sufficient identity.

The same exported remote module imported through different customers,
industries, tenants, or B2B2C paths retains different scoped instance
identities.

Cache, capability, compatibility, and evidence conclusions must not alias
across these paths.

* Capability attenuation

A capability does not cross a domain boundary unchanged.

The resource-owning or delegating domain issues a purpose-bound delegation
according to the agreement.

Delegation binds:

- source and destination domains;
- principal or service audience;
- resource and action scope;
- purpose;
- time and expiry;
- agreement revision;
- delegation depth;
- transfer and re-delegation policy;
- attenuation proof;
- delegation receipt.

Every downstream capability is a subset of its parent authority.

A downstream domain cannot restore removed authority, extend expiry, widen
resource scope, change purpose, or increase delegation depth.

Final action admission remains with the resource-owning domain.

* Multi-hop B2B2C delegation

For an =A -> B -> C= path, admission proves:

- A permits B to delegate further;
- B delegates only a subset to C;
- C principal and domain satisfy A policy;
- all agreement revisions are current;
- every upstream revocation observation is admitted;
- the complete path retains purpose, resource, and confidentiality limits.

B trusting C does not imply that A trusts C.

Transitive trust and re-delegation are disabled by default.

Every hop emits an immutable attenuation and delegation receipt.

* Remote evidence import

Remote evidence is an admission candidate.

The importing domain independently verifies:

- producer and domain identity;
- key binding and trust anchor;
- agreement revision;
- evidence schema;
- semantic subject identity;
- freshness;
- revocation snapshot;
- disclosure projection;
- locally accepted claim role.

A remote compliance claim becomes local input only when the agreement and local
policy admit that exact attestation role.

Remote evidence does not replace local graph-wide compatibility closure unless
the local policy explicitly delegates that proof role.

* Cross-domain time and revocation

Federation does not assume one shared clock.

Cross-domain evidence uses:

- each domain's time observations;
- uncertainty intervals;
- agreement logical versions;
- request and response causal edges;
- lease and fencing authority;
- freshness margins.

Sensitive action policy declares:

- revocation freshness requirement;
- maximum admitted staleness;
- whether an online check is mandatory;
- behavior when the remote domain is unreachable;
- emergency fencing channel.

Unreachable revocation service does not mean no revocation exists.

* Degraded operation

Degraded operation is permitted only when explicitly defined by the agreement.

The policy states:

- which contracts remain available;
- which evidence may become stale;
- which actions are disabled;
- which data remains accessible;
- which capability scopes narrow;
- which recovery or reconciliation obligations arise.

Remote unavailability must not silently weaken verification, data protection,
or capability admission.

A semantic change to degraded behavior creates a new proposal rather than
mutating the existing federated operation.

* Multi-domain governance

Each domain approves obligations it owns.

Examples include:

- data owner approves disclosure;
- resource owner approves action;
- service provider approves service commitment;
- local consumer approves import and use;
- joint policy proves the complete decision set.

No one domain can mint another domain's resource capability.

Joint governance evidence identifies every decision, domain, role, agreement
revision, disclosure projection, and obligation.

* Responsibility chain

Federated receipts identify:

- semantic contract owner;
- evidence producer;
- runtime operator;
- resource owner;
- capability issuer;
- action executor;
- auditor;
- compensation owner.

These roles may belong to different domains.

Partial, failed, unknown, and uncompensatable outcomes retain the exact
responsible roles and next obligations.

A generic federation-failed status is insufficient.

* Federated publication

Every domain owns its active heads.

Cross-domain publication uses ordered transitions, partial receipts, and
convergence by default.

Atomic cross-domain publication may be claimed only when a federation
transaction adapter proves one transaction boundary over the exact participating
resources and authorities.

A global control-plane label is not an atomicity proof.

* Failure taxonomy

The implementation must distinguish at least:

- domain identity unresolved;
- requirement and offer incompatible;
- disclosure projection insufficient;
- agreement revision stale;
- federated module scope collision;
- trust relation missing;
- remote evidence rejected;
- capability attenuation invalid;
- delegation depth exceeded;
- transitive trust attempted;
- upstream revocation stale;
- remote domain unreachable;
- degraded policy unavailable;
- multi-domain quorum incomplete;
- responsibility owner unresolved;
- federated publication partial;
- cross-domain atomicity unproven;
- federation evidence closure incomplete.

* Required invariants

1. Federation has no implicit global root authority.
2. Every domain retains identity, policy, resource, and capability ownership.
3. Requirement and offer close through explicit directional compatibility.
4. Agreement revisions create exact admission boundaries.
5. Cross-domain module identities include agreement and scope path.
6. Capabilities attenuate at every domain boundary.
7. Downstream delegation cannot amplify parent authority.
8. Trust and re-delegation are non-transitive by default.
9. Remote evidence is locally readmitted.
10. Cross-domain clocks and revocation freshness remain explicit.
11. Degraded behavior is declared and cannot silently weaken policy.
12. Every domain approves only obligations it owns.
13. Federated receipts preserve exact responsibility roles.
14. Cross-domain atomicity requires a proven transaction adapter.
15. Public federation extension remains POO-native.
