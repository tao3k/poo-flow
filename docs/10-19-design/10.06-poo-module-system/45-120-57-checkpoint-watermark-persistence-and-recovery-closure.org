* Problem

RFC 56 uses a trusted checkpoint watermark to reject a checkpoint below a
previously observed authority generation.  That proof assumes the watermark
survives a crash or restart without rollback.

Suppose a verifier committed watermark generation =10=.  A checkpoint at
generation =5= is rejected.  If restart resets the watermark to =0=, the same
checkpoint passes the local rollback bound.

#+begin_src typst
#align(center)[
  $
    not "WatermarkAccepted"(W_10, H_5)
      and
    "WatermarkAccepted"(W_0, H_5)
  $
]
#+end_src

Volatile watermark state therefore reintroduces a rollback that the verifier
had already detected.

* Watermark identity

A checkpoint watermark is scoped by:

#+begin_src typst
#align(center)[
  $
    W = (D, A, g_"min")
  $
]
#+end_src

- =D= is the canonical publication-domain identity.
- =A= is the authority identity.
- =g_min= is the minimum acceptable checkpoint generation.

Authority identity alone is insufficient.  Two publication domains owned by
one authority may have independent checkpoint histories.

* Domain identity requires a canonical registry

Equality of an ungoverned numeric identifier does not prove equality of domain
semantics:

#+begin_src typst
#align(center)[
  $
    "id"(D_l) = "id"(D_r)
      and
    "semantics"(D_l) != "semantics"(D_r)
  $
]
#+end_src

The pure publication-domain descriptor is:

#+begin_src typst
#align(center)[
  $
    "Descriptor"(D) = ("id"(D), A(D), S(D))
  $
]
#+end_src

where =S(D)= is the canonical semantic identity of the publication-domain
contract.

A canonical registry makes the identifier injective over registered
descriptors:

#+begin_src typst
#align(center)[
  $
    "Registered"(D_l)
      and
    "Registered"(D_r)
      and
    "id"(D_l) = "id"(D_r)
      "implies"
    "Descriptor"(D_l) = "Descriptor"(D_r)
  $
]
#+end_src

Watermark evidence therefore carries a registered descriptor whose identifier
equals the watermark's publication-domain identity.  Identifier equality
without registry evidence is not accepted as domain equivalence.

Registry membership and domain-identifier equality still do not bind the
watermark to the descriptor's authority.  Identifier-only registration admits
an authority-substitution countermodel:

#+begin_src typst
#align(center)[
  $
    "Registered"(D)
      and
    "id"(D) = D(W)
      and
    A(D) != A(W)
      "implies"
    "IdentityOnlyRegistered"(D, W)
  $
]
#+end_src

Accepted registered-watermark evidence therefore closes both bindings:

#+begin_src typst
#align(center)[
  $
    "RegisteredWatermark"(D, W)
      "implies"
    "id"(D) = D(W)
      and
    A(D) = A(W)
  $
]
#+end_src

* Valid recovery

Let =W_c= be the committed watermark and =W_r= the recovered watermark:

#+begin_src typst
#align(center)[
  $
    "ValidRecovery"(W_c, W_r)
      "iff"
    D(W_c) = D(W_r)
      and
    A(W_c) = A(W_r)
      and
    g_"min"(W_c) <= g_"min"(W_r)
  $
]
#+end_src

Recovery must preserve the publication domain, preserve the authority, and
never decrease the minimum generation.

* Known rollback preservation

If a checkpoint was below the committed watermark, every valid recovered
watermark also rejects it:

#+begin_src typst
#align(center)[
  $
    g("head"(H)) < g_"min"(W_c)
      and
    "ValidRecovery"(W_c, W_r)
      "implies"
    not "WatermarkAccepted"(W_r, H)
  $
]
#+end_src

The theorem preserves previously known rollback evidence across process
restart.  It does not claim that the watermark proves global latestness; RFC 56
still requires authority-issued live-head evidence.

* Recovery evidence

Recovery produces one typed evidence value containing:

- the committed watermark;
- the recovered watermark; and
- the proof that domain, authority, and monotone generation were preserved.

Audit transport may record this evidence.  It cannot manufacture a valid
recovery proof or replace the committed watermark with a lower value.

* Recovery evidence must bind the durable committed value

Internal recovery monotonicity is insufficient when both =committed= and
=recovered= are supplied by the same untrusted payload:

#+begin_src typst
#align(center)[
  $
    "ValidRecovery"(W_0, W_0)
      and
    W_0 != W_"durable"
      and
    g_"min"(W_"durable") > 0
  $
]
#+end_src

The payload is internally consistent while forgetting the actual durable
high-water mark.

A durable recovery authority owns the committed watermark and the issuance
predicate:

#+begin_src typst
#align(center)[
  $
    "Issued"_A(E)
      "implies"
    "committed"(E) = "DurableCommitted"(A)
  $
]
#+end_src

Only authority-issued recovery evidence may produce a recovered watermark.  If
the durable committed value is missing, corrupt, ambiguous, or cannot be bound
to the evidence, recovery returns =Indeterminate=.

#+begin_src typst
#align(center)[
  $
    "RecoveryOutcome" = "Indeterminate"
      "implies"
    not "CheckpointAdmitted"
  $
]
#+end_src

=Indeterminate= is a fail-closed evidence state.  It is not converted into a
zero watermark and does not authorize automatic retry or checkpoint admission.

* Ownership boundary

- The verifier runtime persists the committed watermark.
- The durable recovery authority owns the actual committed watermark and
  recovery-evidence issuance.
- The recovery path reconstructs a watermark only through typed recovery
  evidence.
- Publication-domain identity and authority identity are exact bindings.
- Publication-domain identity is resolved through an injective canonical
  descriptor registry.
- A recovered minimum generation may stay equal or advance; it may not
  decrease.
- Live authority attestation remains responsible for global current-head
  evidence.
- Receipt bytes and audit JSON cannot reset the watermark.

* State machine

#+begin_src mermaid
stateDiagram-v2
  [*] --> WatermarkCommitted: persist domain authority and minimum generation
  WatermarkCommitted --> DomainRegistryChecked: resolve canonical domain descriptor
  DomainRegistryChecked --> AuthorityBindingChecked: descriptor identity is unique
  DomainRegistryChecked --> RecoveryRejected: identity collision or unregistered domain
  AuthorityBindingChecked --> Crash: descriptor authority equals watermark authority
  AuthorityBindingChecked --> RecoveryRejected: authority substitution detected
  Crash --> RecoveryEvidenceLoaded: load committed and recovered watermarks
  RecoveryEvidenceLoaded --> DurableBindingChecked: claimed committed equals durable committed
  DurableBindingChecked --> RecoveryAccepted: identities match and generation does not decrease
  DurableBindingChecked --> Indeterminate: durable binding is missing or ambiguous
  RecoveryEvidenceLoaded --> RecoveryRejected: domain authority or generation regresses
  RecoveryAccepted --> KnownRollbackRejected: old checkpoint remains below watermark
  Indeterminate --> AdmissionRejected: no recovered watermark is issued
  RecoveryRejected --> [*]: fail closed and require repair
  AdmissionRejected --> [*]: explicit reconciliation required
  KnownRollbackRejected --> [*]: live-head validation continues
#+end_src

* Architectural consequence

Checkpoint currentness now has three separate evidence layers:

1. publication-domain membership proves historical publication;
2. authority-issued live-head attestation proves online current selection; and
3. monotone persisted watermark recovery preserves known rollback rejection
   across restart.

None of these layers can substitute for another.
