* Decision

RFC 45-120-44 connects two existing proof owners:

1. RFC 45-120-43 owns complete Cedar response equality, policy freshness,
   credential activity, delegation, separation of duty, and recovery
   commitment binding.
2. RFC 149-01 owns the canonical =AuthorizationSubject= and the proof that two
   valid decision receipts come from distinct engines and distinct receipts.

Neither owner is replaced. The bridge requires both.

For every non-committed recovery position, the exact owner-audit commitment
selects:

- one RFC 45-120-43 lifecycle evidence object;
- one canonical RFC 149-01 authorization subject;
- two RFC 149-01 decision receipts;
- one authoritative Bundle digest.

The bridge admits the position only when the snapshot owner proves that the
exact request, policy set, entity store, and evidence root correspond to the
subject digests; the subject names the authoritative Bundle and current epoch;
the decision receipts close under the existing distinct-engine theorem; and
the lifecycle decision identity binds that exact receipt pair; and the
lifecycle response decision equals the receipt decision.

This is one canonical proof composition. It introduces no alternate
authorization subject or secondary receipt protocol.

* Lean-found architecture gaps

** Full response equality does not identify the authorization subject

The same Cedar response can result from different requests, policy sets, or
entity stores. Therefore response equality alone cannot prove which subject
was evaluated.

#+begin_src typst
$ S := (d_R, d_P, d_E, d_B, t) $

$ r_1 = r_2 "does not imply" S_1 = S_2 $
#+end_src

The independent theorem
=fullResponseEqualityDoesNotIdentifyAuthorizationSubject= constructs two
different subjects with the same complete allow response.

** Two response positions do not prove two engines

The same receipt can be placed in both positions of a weak agreement pair:

#+begin_src typst
$ "weak agreement"(q, q) :=
  "subject"(q) = "subject"(q)
  and "decision"(q) = "decision"(q) $
#+end_src

The theorem =sameEngineReceiptReplayPassesWeakAgreement= proves this weak
replay. The canonical bridge reuses =dualDecisionEvidenceClosed=, which
requires:

#+begin_src typst
$ "engine id"(q_L) != "engine id"(q_R)
  and "receipt id"(q_L) != "receipt id"(q_R) $
#+end_src

=canonicalBridgeRejectsSameEngineReceiptReplay= and
=sameEngineReceiptReplayRejectsBridge= reject the replay in the independent
and actual cores.

** A nonempty decision identity does not bind the receipt pair

RFC 45-120-43 carries one =decisionIdentity=. Without a binding relation, that
identity can name unrelated evidence while the two attached receipts happen to
agree on subject and decision.

#+begin_src typst
$ "decision identity" != emptyset
  "does not imply"
  "dual receipt identity valid"(
    "decision identity", q_L, q_R
  ) $
#+end_src

The independent theorem
=nonemptyDecisionIdentityDoesNotBindDualReceipts= proves the missing
implication. The actual bridge retains
=SourceBoundEffectCompletionRecoveryCedarDualDecisionIdentityValid= as an
owner-supplied relation over the lifecycle identity and exact left/right
receipts.

** Copied digest fields do not prove snapshot authority

Equality between caller-supplied digest strings does not prove that the
snapshot owner computed them from the exact Cedar request, policies, entities,
and evidence root.

#+begin_src typst
$ "field equality"("snapshot", S)
  "does not imply"
  "snapshot binding valid"("snapshot", S) $
#+end_src

The independent theorem =copiedSnapshotFieldsDoNotProveBindingAuthority=
keeps the owner relation independent. The actual type
=SourceBoundEffectCompletionRecoveryCedarSnapshotSubjectBindingValid= consumes
the complete Cedar snapshot and canonical =AuthorizationSubject=.

** A caller-selected Bundle digest is not current-Bundle evidence

Subject equality with an arbitrary function =bundleDigestAt(index)= would only
rename the caller's claim. The trace closure therefore retains a separate
=bundleDigestAtValid= relation:

#+begin_src typst
$ "bundle binding"(i) :=
  "bundle digest valid"(i, d_B(i))
  and "subject bundle"(i) = d_B(i) $
#+end_src

The Bundle evidence owner must validate that digest at the recovery position.

** Digest aliases from different proof domains are not interchangeable

Lean rejected an unqualified =BundleDigest= because the recovery commitment
domain and RFC 149 Bundle evidence domain both expose a digest alias. The
bridge now names =BundleEvidenceBinding.BundleDigest= explicitly.

This is a useful architecture constraint: equal physical representations do
not merge semantic owners.

* Canonical bridge predicate

Let =L= be RFC 45-120-43 lifecycle evidence, =S= the RFC 149-01 subject,
=qL= and =qR= the two decision receipts, =B= the current Bundle digest, =V= the
snapshot-binding owner relation, =D= the decision semantics, and =Q= the
decision-receipt validity relation.

#+begin_src typst
$ "subject bound"(L, S, q_L, q_R, B) :=
  V("snapshot"(L), S)
  and "bundle digest"(S) = B
  and "epoch"(S) = "runtime epoch"(L)
  and "dual decision closed"(D, Q, q_L, q_R)
  and "dual receipt identity valid"(
    "decision identity"(L), q_L, q_R
  )
  and "subject"(q_L) = S
  and "production decision"(L) = "decision"(q_L)
  and D(S) = "reference Cedar decision"(L) $
#+end_src

The complete bridge additionally requires lifecycle admission:

#+begin_src typst
$ "subject bridge closed"(L, S, q_L, q_R, B)
  :=
  "lifecycle admitted"(L)
  and "subject bound"(L, S, q_L, q_R, B) $
#+end_src

The bridge proves:

- both receipts name the exact subject;
- engine identities differ;
- receipt identities differ;
- the subject names the current Bundle and recovery epoch;
- the lifecycle decision equals the independently closed receipt decision;
- the canonical decision semantics equals the imported Cedar reference
  decision for the bound snapshot.
