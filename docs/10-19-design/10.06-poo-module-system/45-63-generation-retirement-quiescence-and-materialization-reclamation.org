#+title: Generation Retirement, Quiescence, and Materialization Reclamation
#+startup: overview

* Status

This RFC records the accepted lifecycle semantics for an immutable generation
after new work stops selecting it.

It extends the publication, activation, drain, and continuation contracts. It
does not introduce mutable lifecycle state inside a generation.

* Central distinction

Retirement stops assigning new work to a generation.

Quiescence proves that admitted execution obligations no longer require the
generation.

Reclamation releases physical materializations and runtime resources.

These are three different decisions with three different evidence boundaries.

The generation remains immutable throughout the lifecycle. Mutable retention
records are external to the generation.

* Typst formalization: retirement, quiescence, and reclamation

Retirement, quiescence, and reclamation are three distinct evidence
boundaries.  Retirement installs a fence after an authoritative active-head
observation proves that the generation no longer receives ordinary new work.

#+begin_src typst
$ H = ActiveHeadObservation(scope, selected-generation, version, receipt) $
$ R = RetirementFence(retiring-generation, H, fence-id, retirement-receipt) $

$ Retire(R)
  => R.retiring-generation != H.selected-generation $
$ Retire(R) => InstallFence(R.fence-id) $
$ OrdinaryNewUse(R.retiring-generation, after: R.fence-id)
  => FailClosed $

$ ActiveHeadObservation(H) not=> Retired(R) $
$ Retired(R) not=> Quiescent(R) $
$ Quiescent(R) not=> Reclaimable(R) $
#+end_src

Quiescence proves the absence of live use pins and outstanding obligations.
Reclamation additionally proves ownership of every physical materialization
being released.  It preserves the immutable semantic generation record.

#+begin_src typst
$ Q = QuiescenceWitness(R, live-pins, obligations, quiescence-receipt) $
$ Quiescent(Q)
  := Q.live-pins = emptyset and Q.obligations = emptyset $

$ M = {MaterializationOwnership(generation, materialization, owner)} $
$ ReclamationAdmission(Q, M)
  => Quiescent(Q) $
$ ReclamationAdmission(Q, M)
  => forall m in M: m.generation = Q.retiring-generation $
$ ReclamationAdmission(Q, M)
  => Present(SemanticGeneration(Q.retiring-generation)) $

$ ConsumerQuiescenceBridge(A, C, Q)
  => A.generation = Q.retiring-generation $
$ ConsumerQuiescenceBridge(A, C, Q)
  => A.consumer in C.required-consumers $
$ ConsumerQuiescenceBridge(A, C, Q)
  => A.pin notin Q.live-pins and C.obligation notin Q.obligations $
#+end_src

The dashed edges deny lifecycle collapse.  Physical reclamation is downstream
of both quiescence and materialization ownership; it never means erasing the
semantic generation or its evidence history.

** Proof obligations

- [X] =headObservationAloneDoesNotEstablishRetirement=: observation is a
  precondition, not the retirement fence.
- [X] =retirementDoesNotEstablishQuiescence=: stopping new work does not prove
  existing use has drained.
- [X] =quiescenceAloneDoesNotAuthorizeReclamation=: zero live obligations does
  not prove ownership of physical materializations.
- [X] =ordinaryNewUseAfterFenceFailsClosed=: ordinary new use is rejected after
  retirement.
- [X] =retirementRequiresAuthoritativeHeadObservation=: retirement carries its
  authoritative active-head observation.
- [X] =retiredGenerationIsNotActiveHead=: the retiring generation differs from
  the observed selected head.
- [X] =quiescenceHasNoLivePins= and
  =quiescenceHasNoOutstandingObligations=: quiescence carries both absence

- [X] =reclamationCarriesMaterializationOwnership=: all reclaimed

* POO semantic ownership

Retention policy is an ordinary higher-order POO object. Native prototype
composition defines:

- reference classification;
- use-pin requirements;
- continuation retention policy;
- reversion retention windows;
- quiescence predicates;
- rematerialization requirements;
- resource reclamation policy;
- receipt disclosure policy.

The public extension interface remains POO-native. Retirement and reclamation
must not become enum tokens, an ad hoc garbage-collection DSL, or raw mutable
callbacks.

A pure =RetentionPolicy= may produce proposals. It has no authority to delete
an artifact, terminate a flow, expire a continuation, or release a remote
resource.

* Retirement

Retirement begins only after an authoritative active-head observation proves
that the generation no longer receives ordinary new flows for the relevant
publication scope.

A =RetirementProposal= identifies:

- semantic generation identity;
- publication and consumer scopes;
- superseding active-head version;
- retirement fence version;
- retention policy identity;
- observed live-reference set identity.

Authorization installs a retirement fence. After that fence, ordinary new use
pins for the retired generation fail closed.

Policy may still authorize a narrowly scoped pin for:

- continuation resumption;
- reconciliation;
- explicit reversion preparation;
- evidence-preserving reproduction;
- authorized migration.

Such exceptions are explicit capabilities. They are not a bypass around the
retirement fence.

* Semantic and executable references

A semantic reference requires retention of identity and proof surfaces.

An executable reference requires the generation materialization to remain
loadable or reproducibly rematerializable.

Semantic references include:

- generation and revision identities;
- dependency closure digests;
- publication and activation receipts;
- transition and reversion history;
- build and materialization evidence;
- reclamation evidence.

Executable references may include:

- active or draining flows;
- suspended continuations;
- prepared activation candidates;
- pending effects;
- unknown commits and reconciliation;
- open reversion windows;
- runtime generation-use leases;
- explicit audit or reproduction obligations.

A historical receipt does not, by itself, require permanent residency of every
executable artifact.

* Generation-use pins

Every live executable dependency holds a versioned generation-use pin.

A pin records:

- generation identity;
- holder and flow identity;
- reference role;
- acquisition fence version;
- lease and renewal evidence;
- capability scope;
- release or expiry evidence.

Retirement first fences new ordinary pins. Existing pins then drain, migrate,
expire, or resolve under their admitted policies.

Quiescence requires an authoritative observation of the complete relevant pin
set. A local process scan is not sufficient proof of global quiescence.

The reclamation intent performs a final compare-and-swap over the retention
record and fence version. This closes the race between quiescence observation
and a newly authorized exceptional pin.

* Continuation retention

A suspended continuation remains an executable generation reference even when
no process stack is currently running.

Continuation retention evidence contains:

- continuation identity;
- captured semantic generation identity;
- flow identity;
- retention lease;
- expiry and renewal policy;
- compatible migration evidence when available;
- resumability receipt.

Lease expiry alone does not silently destroy continuation semantics. Expiry
allows the retention policy to propose expiration, migration, archival, or
termination. The selected action still requires its own authority and receipt.

* Continuable materialization failure

When a continuation is resumed but its generation materialization is
unavailable, the Scheme runtime should raise a continuable condition such as
=GenerationMaterializationUnavailable=.

Admissible restart roles may include:

- rematerialize from content-addressed storage;
- migrate after compatibility proof;
- defer resumption and reacquire authority;
- terminate the flow explicitly.

First-class continuations preserve the recoverable control position. The POO
retention and migration policies determine which restart is semantically
admissible.

A restart must not bypass:

- capability admission;
- resource budgets;
- generation compatibility proof;
- migration authority;
- receipt production.

* Quiescence

A pure =QuiescenceProposal= references a closed retention evidence set.

The evidence set must cover at least:

- all active-head and candidate-head references;
- active and draining flow pins;
- suspended continuation pins;
- prepared activation candidates;
- pending effects and unknown outcomes;
- open reconciliation obligations;
- admitted reversion windows;
- runtime and adapter leases;
- policy-required reproduction obligations.

Missing, stale, or globally incomplete reference evidence produces an
incomplete quiescence result. It must not be interpreted as zero references.

* Reclamation eligibility

A pure =ReclamationProposal= may be produced only after quiescence and the
retention policy both admit reclamation.

The proposal identifies:

- generation identity;
- materialization identities;
- exact resource scopes;
- quiescence evidence closure;
- retention policy identity;
- rematerialization proof status;
- destructive capability requirements.

The proposal is not a delete operation.

* Materialization and semantic history

Reclamation removes a materialization or runtime resource. It does not remove
semantic history.

The system retains:

- generation and definition identities;
- dependency closure digests;
- build and materialization receipts;
- publication and activation history;
- retirement and quiescence evidence;
- reclamation proposal, authorization, and receipts.

If the admitted source closure and deterministic build evidence remain
available, the same semantic generation may be rematerialized.

Rematerialization does not create a new semantic generation when content
identity is proven equal. If reproduced content cannot prove the same identity,
it receives a new materialization identity and must not impersonate the
reclaimed artifact.

* Destructive authority

Physical reclamation is a destructive live action.

Every resource action requires:

- an exact resource identity and scope;
- an admitted reclamation proposal;
- a destructive resource capability;
- current fence and retention versions;
- an idempotency identity;
- a resource-level reclamation receipt.

Partial reclamation and unknown outcomes are first-class results. An unknown
outcome enters reconciliation before the resource is retried or considered
available.

A single global garbage-collection success flag must not hide resource-level
outcomes.

* Failure taxonomy

The implementation must distinguish at least:

- retirement observation incomplete;
- retirement fence conflict;
- unauthorized exceptional use pin;
- stale or incomplete pin-set evidence;
- continuation lease unresolved;
- continuation materialization unavailable;
- migration proof rejected;
- pending effect or reconciliation obligation;
- reversion window still open;
- quiescence evidence incomplete;
- missing destructive capability;
- reclamation compare-and-swap conflict;
- partial resource reclamation;
- unknown reclamation outcome;
- rematerialization identity mismatch.

* Required invariants

1. Retirement, quiescence, and reclamation remain separate decisions.
2. The semantic generation remains immutable.
3. Retirement fences new ordinary use pins before drain.
4. Every executable dependency has a versioned use pin.
5. Suspended continuations remain executable references.
6. Missing reference evidence never means zero references.
7. Quiescence covers the complete admitted reference scope.
8. Reclamation performs a final retention-record compare-and-swap.
9. Continuable restarts cannot bypass policy or authority.
10. Reclamation removes materialization, not semantic history.
11. Rematerialization must prove content identity.
12. Destructive actions are resource-scoped and receipted.
13. Partial and unknown reclamation outcomes remain visible.
14. Public semantic extension remains POO-native.
