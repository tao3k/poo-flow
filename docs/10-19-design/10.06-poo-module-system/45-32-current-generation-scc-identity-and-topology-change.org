#+title: Current-Generation SCC Identity and Topology Change
#+status: Accepted
#+language: en

* Decision

A fixed-point strongly connected component has no timeless identity.

Its authoritative identity is derived from the slot-demand topology actually
observed and sealed in the current contribution generation.  A predecessor
generation's SCC may guide discovery and scheduling, but it cannot authorize
current-generation reuse, convergence or recovery.

An SCC identity therefore means:

#+begin_quote
this causally complete group of mutually dependent POO slot demands, with
these internal edges and semantic obligations, as observed in this generation
#+end_quote

It does not mean a permanent numeric component identifier, a source-level
module group or a cache namespace.

* Native POO boundary

=gerbil-poo= remains the sole implementation of POO objects, prototype
composition, slot demand and open recursion.

POO Flow derives SCC evidence from native demand.  It MUST NOT add:

- a second fixed-point evaluator;
- a static module dependency graph presented as runtime truth;
- public SCC declarations;
- user-assigned component identifiers;
- raw graph patches or cache-key annotations.

SCC discovery is an observation of POO semantics, not a new composition
language.

* Provisional and sealed topology

During evaluation, the demand graph is provisional.  New native slot demand
may add an edge, reveal a back edge, join previously separate components or
show that a predecessor edge is no longer demanded.

An SCC becomes sealed for the current generation only when:

1. every currently demanded member has been observed under the same generation;
2. internal demand edges have been recorded from actual evaluation;
3. the demand frontier relevant to the component is closed or explicitly
   suspended;
4. slot contracts, priority metadata, capability and authority obligations are
   known;
5. convergence or an authorized continuable condition has produced a receipt;
6. the observation and convergence budgets have not been bypassed.

Sealing is relative to the demanded semantic closure.  POO Flow does not force
every syntactically reachable branch merely to construct a global static graph.

flowchart LR
  G["Current contribution generation"] -->|"starts from an invalidated demand frontier"| P["Provisional demand graph"]
  H["Predecessor footprint"] -->|"offers scheduling hints without reuse authority"| P
  P -->|"native slot demand discovers an ordinary edge"| P
  P -->|"a back edge reveals mutual dependency"| W["Provisional cycle witness"]
  W -->|"expands observation to the complete demanded component"| C["Current SCC candidate"]
  C -->|"new demand changes membership or internal edges"| P
  C -->|"frontier, obligations and convergence are complete"| S["Sealed current-generation SCC"]

* SCC identity commitment

A sealed SCC identity MUST commit to:

| Dimension | Required commitment |
|-----------+---------------------|
| Generation | The contribution generation in which the topology was observed |
| Members | Canonical identities of every member slot demand |
| Internal edges | Canonical causal demand edges among members |
| Boundary dependencies | External demanded values consumed by the component |
| Contribution closure | Active, equal-priority and suppressed contributions relevant to members |
| Contracts and priority | Retained contract objects, priority classes and force authority |
| Capability and authority | Ownership and authority scopes governing every cross-object demand |
| Nested lineage | Submodule identity and prototype lineage for members and edges |
| Budgets | Observation and convergence budgets used to seal the component |
| Convergence evidence | Result, cycle condition or authorized suspension applying to the whole component |

Process addresses, traversal order, source timestamps, graph-algorithm indices
and physical cache locations MUST NOT participate in semantic identity.

* Topology transitions between generations

The relationship between predecessor and current SCCs is evidence, not
identity inheritance.

| Transition | Current-generation rule |
|------------+-------------------------|
| Unchanged | Reuse remains only a candidate until members, edges, obligations and boundary dependencies are reconfirmed |
| Growth | The expanded SCC is recomputed and sealed as one component |
| Shrink | Removed members and edges are invalidated; the remaining component is independently resealed |
| Merge | All predecessor SCCs and newly connecting demands form one recomputation closure |
| Split | Each resulting SCC or acyclic demand receives independent validation; the predecessor result does not divide automatically |
| Appearance | A newly observed cycle creates a new SCC with no predecessor reuse claim |
| Dissolution | Former members become acyclic or enter other SCCs and are evaluated under their new closures |

  O -->|"member or internal edge disappears"| H["Shrink"]
  O -->|"new edges connect predecessor components"| M["Merge"]

* Split and merge safety

** Split

When one predecessor SCC becomes several current components, predecessor
results cannot simply be partitioned by member.

The old result was justified by convergence of the old mutual dependency
closure.  Each new component must prove:

- which old cross-component edges are absent in the current generation;
- which boundary dependencies it now consumes;
- whether its contribution and contract closure remains equivalent;
- whether its result can be reused independently;
- which predecessor receipt supplies causal lineage.

An individual result may eventually be reused, but only after the new component
has established independence.

** Merge

When current demand edges connect several predecessor SCCs into a single SCC,
no predecessor component may be treated as independently converged.

The complete merged member set is one fixed-point recomputation closure.
Observation budgets may be delegated among submodules, but convergence and the
final receipt apply to the whole merged SCC.

* Cross-submodule SCCs

Module and submodule boundaries do not arbitrarily cut a real dependency
cycle.  If native demand produces mutual reachability across nested submodules,
the sealed SCC may cross those boundaries.

Every cross-boundary edge still retains:

- source and target submodule identity;
- prototype lineage;
- imported capability identity;
- delegated authority and budget;
- causal demand receipt.

This preserves nested ownership without pretending that a cycle disappears at
a namespace boundary.

flowchart LR
  A["Submodule A slot"] -->|"demands an imported capability from B"| B["Submodule B slot"]
  B -->|"demands a derived value from C"| C["Nested submodule C slot"]
  C -->|"demands A through authorized prototype lineage"| A
  A -->|"all three demands are mutually reachable"| S["Cross-submodule SCC"]
  B -->|"edge retains capability and authority evidence"| S
  C -->|"edge retains nested identity and delegated budget"| S
  S -->|"converges or raises one causally complete condition"| R["Component receipt"]
#+end_src

* Dependency changes during convergence

A slot computation may reveal different dependencies as provisional values
change.  Therefore SCC membership may change while the fixed point is being
observed.

POO Flow MUST:

1. record topology revisions in the current generation;
2. revoke any provisional convergence claim invalidated by a new edge;
3. enlarge, split or merge the recomputation closure as required;
4. charge the additional observation work to the active budget;
5. retain the causal edge that triggered the topology revision;
6. seal only the final observed component topology.

Repeated topology oscillation is an observable failure mode.  It consumes the
convergence budget and may raise a continuable condition with the topology
revision history as evidence.

* Recovery and generation change

A continuation captured for a provisional or sealed SCC is bound to its
generation and topology evidence.

If recovery adds a contribution, changes authority, refines a dependency or
adjusts an authorized budget, resumption enters a new contribution generation.
The predecessor SCC becomes a discovery hint and causal predecessor only.

flowchart LR
  E["SCC cycle, oscillation or budget condition"] -->|"captures current topology and causal witness"| K["Generation-bound continuation"]
  K -->|"authorized recovery changes semantic input"| N["New generation"]
  N -->|"uses the predecessor SCC only to seed observation"| P["Fresh provisional topology"]
  P -->|"reconfirms, splits, grows or merges members"| S["New sealed SCC"]
  S -->|"links without inheriting authority"| R["Successor receipt"]
#+end_src

* SCC lineage receipt

The successor receipt SHOULD relate current and predecessor components through
POO evidence objects describing:

- equivalent topology candidate;
- growth or shrinkage;
- merge or split;
- appearance or dissolution;
- predecessor receipt identities;
- member and edge additions or removals;
- changed boundary dependencies;
- changed contracts, priority, capability or authority;
- recomputation and reuse decisions;
- topology revisions consumed during convergence.

These relations are evidence projections.  They MUST NOT become an ad hoc
public enum registry or a user-authored transition DSL.

* Required failure conditions

Evaluation MUST fail or enter an authorized continuable condition when:

- a predecessor SCC identity is used as current-generation proof;
- current membership or internal edges cannot be sealed;
- a split reuses a predecessor result without proving independence;
- a merge preserves separate convergence claims;
- a cross-submodule edge lacks capability or authority evidence;
- topology revisions exceed the observation or convergence budget;
- a cache entry is keyed by provisional rather than sealed SCC identity;
- a resumption attempts to continue under stale generation or topology
  authority.

* Acceptance checklist

- [X] SCC identity is derived and sealed per contribution generation.
- [X] Predecessor SCCs are discovery hints and causal evidence, not current
  proof.
- [X] SCC identity includes members, internal edges, boundary dependencies and
  semantic obligations.
- [X] Growth, shrink, merge, split, appearance and dissolution have explicit
  recomputation rules.
- [X] A split must prove the independence of each successor closure.
- [X] A merge is recomputed as one fixed-point closure.
- [X] SCCs may cross submodule boundaries while retaining nested authority and
  identity evidence.
- [X] Topology revisions during convergence are observable and budgeted.
- [X] Recovery enters a new generation and rediscovers topology.
- [X] Only sealed SCC identity may authorize cache reuse.
- [ ] Validate the model with executable unchanged, growth, shrink, merge,
  split, cross-submodule, topology-oscillation and recovery scenarios before
  implementation begins.
