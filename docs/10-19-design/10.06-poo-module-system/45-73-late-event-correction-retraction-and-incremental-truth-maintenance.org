#+title: Late-Event Correction, Retraction, and Incremental Truth Maintenance
#+startup: overview

* Status

This RFC records the accepted truth-maintenance semantics for late events,
corrections, retractions, and effects derived from earlier causal cuts.

It extends causal event ingress, incremental fixed-point reevaluation, active
selection, continuations, and effect compensation.

* Typst formalization: immutable history and conclusion revision

A late event never mutates an old proof into an error.  The old conclusion
remains historical evidence for its immutable causal cut; reevaluation creates
a new cut and, when the conclusion changes, a new conclusion identity.

#+begin_src typst
#let conclusion-id(cut, policy) = (cut, policy)

$ late-event(event, old-cut)
  ==> new-cut = admit(old-cut, event)
  and new-cut.identity != old-cut.identity $

$ historical-conclusion(c)
  ==> immutable(c.proof)
  and immutable(c.cut)
  and not active(c) $

$ new-cut.identity != old-cut.identity
  ==> conclusion-id(new-cut.identity, policy)
  != conclusion-id(old-cut.identity, policy) $
#+end_src

Monotone derivation preserves the previous reachable frontier.  Non-monotone
derivation explicitly invalidates every affected conclusion and selects one
eligible active revision.  Retraction marks a conclusion inactive but does not
delete its proof or effect receipts.

#+begin_src typst
$ monotone(policy)
  ==> reachable(old-cut) subset.eq reachable(new-cut) $

$ non-monotone(policy)
  ==> forall(
    conclusion in affected(new-cut),
    conclusion in invalidated(new-cut),
  ) $

$ retraction(receipt, conclusion)
  ==> inactive(conclusion)
  and preserve(conclusion.historical-proof)
  and preserve(conclusion.effect-receipts) $

$ active-selection(candidates)
  ==> exists.unique(c in candidates, eligible(c) and active(c)) $

$ compensation-proposal(p)
  ==> not action-authority(p) $
#+end_src

* Central decision

An old proof is not mutated into an error.

It remains evidence that a conclusion followed from its original immutable
causal cut and policy.

A late event creates a new causal cut, a new evaluation, and, when required, a
new conclusion revision.

Historical conclusions, proofs, and effect receipts remain immutable.

* POO semantic ownership

Truth-maintenance policy is represented by ordinary higher-order POO objects.
Native prototype composition defines:

- monotonicity;
- correction and retraction semantics;
- watermark and coverage requirements;
- active-conclusion selection;
- compensation policy;
- continuation migration;
- scoped finality.

The public extension interface remains POO-native. Correction, finality,
retraction, and compensation must not become unstructured macro tokens or
mutable callbacks.

* Derived conclusion provenance

Every derived conclusion records:

- conclusion identity;
- generation and policy identities;
- causal event-cut identity;
- resource, time, and entropy observation identities;
- dependency demand manifest;
- graph and SCC identities;
- proof, violation, or suspension identity;
- effect intents and receipts derived from the conclusion;
- conclusion revision and supersession edges;
- active-conclusion selection evidence.

This provenance supports exact reverse-dependency invalidation when a late
event creates a new cut.

* Monotone derivation

A monotone policy proves that adding admitted facts cannot invalidate an
existing conclusion.

Monotone derivation may reuse prior proofs and compute only the newly reachable
incremental frontier.

Monotonicity is a property proved for the exact policy and contract domain. It
must not be inferred merely because an implementation uses set-like data.

* Non-monotone derivation

Non-monotone rules include:

- absence assertions;
- counts, averages, and top-k results;
- latest-value selection;
- uniqueness;
- negation;
- closed-world assumptions;
- watermark-closed aggregates;
- winner selection whose candidate set can change.

A non-monotone policy defines explicit correction and retraction semantics.

The evaluator must not assume that insertion-only incremental propagation is
sufficient for a non-monotone SCC.

* Correction

A correction applies when a conclusion remains meaningful for the same domain
subject but its value, scope, or proof changes under the new cut.

A =CorrectionProposal= records:

- prior conclusion and cut identities;
- new conclusion and cut identities;
- changed premise identities;
- affected dependency frontier;
- policy and generation identities;
- exact supersession relation;
- required active-pointer and downstream actions.

The new conclusion uses a =supersedes= edge. It does not overwrite the prior
conclusion.

* Retraction

A retraction applies when the prior conclusion is no longer derivable under the
new admitted cut.

A =RetractionProof= records:

- prior conclusion and proof;
- prior and new cut identities;
- added, removed, or corrected premise evidence;
- invalidated dependency edge;
- monotonicity or non-monotonicity policy;
- graph and SCC path;
- exact causal explanation.

Deleting a row, cache entry, or presentation record is not a retraction proof.

Retraction removes current applicability through a new selection transition.
It does not erase historical evidence.

* Scoped finality

Finality is always scoped.

A finality claim identifies:

- source set;
- sequence and partition coverage;
- watermark identity;
- observation coverage;
- policy revision;
- generation and cut identities;
- late-event authority and policy.

New sources, stronger late-event authority, corrected provenance, or a new
policy revision may create a new conclusion even after an earlier scoped
finality proof.

The implementation must not advertise unconditional permanent finality when
its admitted evidence only proves bounded finality.

* Active conclusion pointer

Conclusions and proofs remain immutable.

A versioned external active-conclusion pointer selects the currently admitted
revision for a domain subject and scope.

Pointer updates use:

- expected current version;
- selected conclusion or retraction identity;
- correction or retraction evidence;
- selection capability;
- compare-and-swap;
- transition receipt.

A compare-and-swap conflict requires fresh observation and reevaluation. It
must not use last-write-wins.

* Effects and compensation

A new cut cannot undo an already committed external effect.

If policy admits compensation, a new =CompensationProposal= references:

- original conclusion and effect receipt;
- correction or retraction proof;
- current external resource observation;
- compensation intent;
- new idempotency identity;
- required capability;
- reconciliation policy.

Compensation is a new effect with its own authorization and receipt.

It is not reverse execution or mutation of the original effect history.

When an effect cannot be compensated, the system emits
=UncompensatableEffectEvidence= and routes it through explicit governance or
domain policy.

The system must not report automatic recovery success for an uncompensatable
effect.

* Incremental truth-maintenance index

The truth-maintenance index records:

- conclusion-to-premise dependency edges;
- premise-to-demand and premise-to-SCC reverse edges;
- monotonicity proof identity;
- watermark and coverage dependencies;
- effect dependencies;
- correction and retraction chains;
- active-conclusion pointer identity.

Index entries accelerate canonical reevaluation. They do not replace immutable
source events, causal cuts, or proof evidence.

An incomplete reverse index must not be used to claim that a late event affects
no conclusions.

* Absence and negative facts

Absence is admitted only when evidence closes:

- source scope;
- partition and sequence coverage;
- sequence gaps;
- watermark and uncertainty;
- late-event policy;
- observation coverage contract.

Without this closure, absence remains suspension.

Failure to observe an event is not proof that the event did not occur.

* Non-monotone cyclic SCCs

Insertion, correction, and retraction may destabilize a non-monotone SCC.

The affected SCC:

- reevaluates as one joint obligation;
- records insertion and retraction causal paths;
- uses a bounded evaluation budget;
- emits a new SCC evidence identity after stabilization;
- emits suspension for oscillation, unproven monotonicity, or budget
  exhaustion.

The last transient result of an unstable SCC must not become the active
conclusion.

* Continuations and changing cuts

A suspended continuation remains bound to its original:

- semantic generation;
- causal event cut;
- dependency manifest;
- conclusion revision;
- pending effects and acknowledgements.

It may finish pure evaluation for that original cut.

Crossing a current admission boundary requires comparison with the latest
active cut and active conclusion.

Moving the continuation to a new cut requires an explicit migration proposal,
compatibility proof, authority, and migration receipt.

The runtime must not silently inject late events into a captured continuation.

* Completion boundaries

The following completions remain distinct:

- late event admitted;
- new causal cut closed;
- incremental reevaluation stabilized;
- correction or retraction published;
- active-conclusion pointer updated;
- compensation committed or reconciled;
- downstream consumers activated.

No earlier completion implies a later completion.

* Failure taxonomy

The implementation must distinguish at least:

- late event provenance rejected;
- affected provenance frontier incomplete;
- monotonicity unproven;
- non-monotone reevaluation suspended;
- correction proposal incomplete;
- retraction proof incomplete;
- scoped finality exceeded;
- active-pointer compare-and-swap conflict;
- prior effect already committed;
- compensation unauthorized;
- compensation partial or unknown;
- effect uncompensatable;
- absence coverage incomplete;
- SCC oscillation;
- continuation cut migration rejected;
- downstream activation incomplete.

* Required invariants

1. Historical proofs and conclusions remain immutable.
2. A late event creates a new causal cut.
3. Monotone and non-monotone policies remain distinct.
4. Correction supersedes rather than overwrites.
5. Retraction requires a proof and does not erase history.
6. Finality is scoped to exact evidence and policy.
7. Current applicability uses a versioned active-conclusion pointer.
8. Pointer conflicts require fresh observation.
9. Committed effects are not reversed by proof mutation.
10. Compensation is a new authorized effect.
11. Uncompensatable effects remain explicit.
12. Absence requires closed source, sequence, watermark, and coverage evidence.
13. Non-monotone SCCs stabilize before publication.
14. Continuations remain bound to their original cut.
15. Public truth-maintenance policy extension remains POO-native.
