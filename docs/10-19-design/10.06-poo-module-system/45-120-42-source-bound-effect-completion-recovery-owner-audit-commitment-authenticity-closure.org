* Logic gaps found by Lean

** A non-empty witness identity did not determine its payload

45-120-41 required a non-empty =witnessId=.  Two distinct audit payloads could
still be assigned the same arbitrary non-empty string.

#+begin_src typst
$
  P_0 != P_1
  quad and quad
  w != epsilon
  quad "does not imply" quad
  "payload"(w) = P_0
$
#+end_src

The independent theorem
=nonemptyWitnessIdentityDoesNotDeterminePayload= supplies two payloads that
differ only in the resulting owner receipt and remain compatible with one
arbitrary non-zero identity.

** A digest equality did not prove collision resistance

The recovery engine cannot prove cryptographic collision resistance merely by
calling a value a digest.  The commitment scheme must expose an explicit
injectivity contract over the canonical audit payload.

#+begin_src typst
$
  C(P) = C(Q) arrow P = Q
$
#+end_src

The theorem =collisionResistanceRejectsPayloadSubstitution= uses this premise
to reject a changed payload under the same commitment.

This is an abstract proof contract.  It is not an implementation claim about a
particular hash algorithm.

** Content addressing did not imply authorization

A correct content commitment proves what was committed.  It does not prove
that an enterprise authority was allowed to authorize it.

#+begin_src typst
$
  c = C(P)
  quad "does not imply" quad
  "Authorized"(a, c)
$
#+end_src

The independent theorem =contentCommitmentDoesNotProduceAuthorization= keeps
these premises separate.

** Signature verification did not imply authorization

A credential may verify a signature while the signing authority lacks the
required policy permission.

#+begin_src typst
$
  "Verified"(k, c, sigma)
  quad "does not imply" quad
  "Authorized"(a, c)
$
#+end_src

=verifiedSignatureDoesNotImplyAuthorization= proves this counterexample with a
verification relation that is true and an authorization relation that is
false.

** Authentication did not identify enterprise responsibility

An authority and credential do not by themselves identify who is accountable
or which duty scope is accepted.  Enterprise authenticity evidence must carry
both =accountabilityIdentity= and =responsibilityScopeDigest=.

* Decision

Define a canonical commitment payload containing:

- the previous commitment;
- ordinal;
- recovery ID;
- exact recovery owner;
- owner identity;
- before and after owner receipt IDs;
- runtime epoch;
- active fence token.

#+begin_src typst
$
  P_i :=
  (
    c_(i - 1),
    n_i,
    r_i,
    o_i,
    a_i,
    b_i,
    e_i,
    u_i,
    f_i
  )
$
#+end_src

Define =witnessId= as the commitment of this complete payload:

#+begin_src typst
$
  c_i = C(P_i)
  quad and quad
  "witness-id"_i = c_i
$
#+end_src

Every payload field is also equal to the corresponding canonical audit witness
field.  Because 45-120-41 already binds that witness to the exact native owner
receipt, the commitment transitively covers the native transition without
duplicating native transition semantics.

* Commitment chain

For adjacent non-committed positions:

#+begin_src typst
$
  P_(i + 1)."previous-commitment" = "some"(c_i)
$
#+end_src

=adjacentCommitmentsPreservePredecessor= proves this from:

- the 45-120-41 audit predecessor edge;
- current and next commitment closures;
- the equality between each witness ID and its commitment.

The theorem =commitmentClosedRejectsPayloadSubstitution= proves that two closed
envelopes for the same witness under one collision-resistant scheme have equal
payloads.

* Enterprise authenticity evidence

The canonical evidence contains:

- exact commitment;
- authority identity;
- credential identity;
- policy decision identity;
- policy evidence root;
- accountability identity;
- responsibility scope digest;
- signature.

Its validity is:

#+begin_src typst
$
  "Authentic"(E, c)
  :=
  E."commitment" = c
  and "Authorized"(E."authority", c)
  and "Verified"(E."credential", c, E."signature")
$
#+end_src

All identity, evidence-root, accountability, responsibility-scope, and
signature fields must also be non-empty.

* Ownership boundary

The recovery proof accepts two external relations:

- =authorized : AuthorityIdentity -> Commitment -> Prop=;
- =signatureVerified : CredentialIdentity -> Commitment -> Signature -> Prop=.

The recovery engine does not:

- evaluate Cedar policies;
- choose between the two policy engines;
- validate credentials;
- implement signature algorithms;
- issue policy decisions;
- infer accountability or responsibility.

Those owners must supply evidence for the exact commitment.  This RFC only
proves that admitted recovery evidence cannot detach those decisions from the
committed native transition.

* Actual closure

=SourceBoundEffectCompletionRecoveryOwnerAuditCommitmentEvidence= contains:

- the complete 45-120-41 owner-audit evidence;
- a commitment and authenticity closure for every non-committed position.

The proof projection remains:

#+begin_src typst

  "commitment-authenticity"
  arrow
  "owner-audit"
  arrow
  "owner-coverage"
  arrow
  "progress-evidence"
  arrow

$
#+end_src

=closedCommitmentAuditBuildsOwnerAudit= performs the first projection.
=closedCommitmentAuditConverges= reuses the unchanged recovery convergence
semantics.

* Fail-closed boundaries

The canonical core and actual closure reject:

- payload substitution under one witness commitment;
- a broken previous-commitment edge;
- authenticity evidence bound to another commitment;
- an unauthorized authority;
- an invalid signature;
- an empty accountability identity.

The corresponding theorems include:

- =commitmentClosedRejectsPayloadSubstitution=;
- =mismatchedAuthenticityCommitmentRejectsClosure=;
- =unauthorizedCommitmentRejectsAuthenticityClosure=;
- =invalidSignatureRejectsAuthenticityClosure=;
- =mismatchedCommitmentRejectsCommitmentAudit=;
- =unauthorizedCommitmentRejectsCommitmentAudit=;
- =invalidSignatureRejectsCommitmentAudit=.

No theorem repairs or defaults missing evidence.

* Next proof gap

=authorized= and =signatureVerified= are still abstract relations.  The next
proof must bind them to current policy and credential lifecycle evidence:

- the Cedar dual-engine decision and agreement or divergence result;
- exact policy snapshot and policy evidence root;
- credential validity interval, rotation, and revocation state;
- delegated authority and separation-of-duty constraints;
- decision freshness relative to the recovery epoch and active fence.

That proof must keep policy ownership outside the recovery engine while making
stale, revoked, divergent, or unaccountable authorization fail closed.
