#+title: Admission, Fairness, Backpressure, and Scheduling Isolation
#+startup: overview

* Status

This RFC records the accepted resource admission, fairness, and backpressure
semantics for local and distributed POO Flow evaluation.

It extends distributed evaluator delegation. It does not change POO slot
contribution priority.

* Typst formalization: conserved admission and isolated scheduling

Resource admission is separate from semantic slot resolution.  Scheduling may
choose which admitted demand receives finite resources first, but it cannot
change the POO contribution selected by semantic composition.

#+begin_src typst
#let consumes(outcome) = outcome == "admitted"
#let backpressure(outcome) = (
  outcome == "queued"
  or outcome == "suspended"
  or outcome == "rejected"
)

$ scheduled-resolution(d, decision)
  = semantic-resolution(d) $

$ outcome(d) != "admitted" ==> not consumes(outcome(d)) $

$ outcome(d) in ("queued", "suspended", "rejected")
  ==> backpressure(outcome(d)) $
#+end_src

Admission conserves hierarchical capacity and tenant reservations.  Fairness is
conditional: a continuously eligible demand with finite eligible competition
is eventually scheduled only when the scheduler itself continues to make
progress.

#+begin_src typst
#let conserved(parent, dimension) = (
  parent.admitted-child-aggregate.at(dimension)
  <= parent.capacity.at(dimension)
)

#let isolated(tenant, dimension) = (
  tenant.consumed.at(dimension)
  <= tenant.reserved.at(dimension)
)

$ forall(dim, conserved(parent, dim)) $

$ forall(tenant, dim, isolated(tenant, dim)) $

$ continuously-eligible(d)
  and finite-eligible-competition(d)
  and scheduler-progress()
  ==> eventually-scheduled(d) $

$ queue-depth(q) <= queue-bound(q) $
#+end_src

* Central separation

POO slot contribution priority decides which semantic contribution wins during
slot resolution.

Runtime scheduling policy decides which admitted demand receives finite
execution resources first.

These are independent systems.

Slot priority must not grant CPU, memory, queue position, preemption rights,
tenant quota, or broader admission authority.

* Semantic contribution priority

The established semantic priority model remains:

- normal contribution priority is =0=;
- larger numeric values provide stronger semantic precedence;
- =default= is below normal contributions;
- =force= is above ordinary contributions.

These values participate only in POO contribution resolution.

After semantic resolution, scheduling receives the resolved demand and its
explicit scheduling policy. It must not infer scheduling urgency from the
winning contribution priority.

* Scheduling policy objects

Runtime scheduling uses distinct higher-order POO objects such as:

- =ServiceClass=;
- =FairShareWeight=;
- =DeadlineObservation=;
- =ResourceBudget=;
- =PreemptionPolicy=;
- =AdmissionScope=.

These objects describe execution policy. They do not alter the semantic demand
identity or its canonical pure result.

The public extension interface remains POO-native. Scheduling must not become
an unstructured queue-number or macro-token DSL.

* Admission boundary

Admission separates a pure proposal from a live resource reservation.

A pure =AdmissionProposal= projects:

- demand identity;
- resource requirements;
- admission scope;
- service and fairness policy;
- deadline and freshness observations;
- placement requirements;
- checkpoint and preemption requirements.

The proposal has no authority to reserve or consume resources.

A live reservation requires current resource evidence, scoped reservation
authority, and compare-and-swap against the resource ledger.

* Hierarchical resource conservation

Resource reservation is hierarchical:

#+begin_example
host capacity
  -> tenant allocation
    -> composition-root allocation
      -> SCC allocation
        -> demand allocation
#+end_example

A child allocation consumes resources already admitted by its parent scope.

Retry, remote delegation, demand expansion, checkpoint resumption, and worker
migration must not recreate the parent budget.

The resource ledger records reservation, consumption, release, expiry, and
reconciliation evidence at every hierarchy edge.

* Resource dimensions

Admission may account independently for:

- CPU time;
- memory;
- local and remote concurrency;
- network transfer;
- storage and materialization;
- external service quota;
- evaluation demand count;
- continuation and checkpoint retention;
- observability volume.

Availability in one dimension does not compensate for a failed hard
requirement in another dimension unless an explicit POO policy proves the
substitution.

* Fairness scopes

Fairness policy covers at least:

- tenants;
- composition roots;
- independent SCC frontiers;
- interactive and batch workloads;
- long-waiting demands;
- confidentiality-constrained placement domains.

A concrete implementation may use weighted fair queueing, deficit accounting,
or another admitted scheduler algorithm.

The algorithm's internal queue identifiers are not public semantic identities.

Fairness evidence records the policy and scope under which a selection was
made.

* Bounded urgency donation

A high-urgency demand may depend on work admitted under a lower service class.

The scheduler may grant bounded urgency donation when:

- an exact causal dependency edge exists;
- the parent demand is already admitted;
- the donation does not exceed the parent's admitted service class;
- resource and confidentiality placement remain valid;
- donation ends when the dependency resolves or the parent is cancelled.

Donation does not change dependency semantic identity or permanently elevate
its service class.

Every donation emits a receipt identifying donor, recipient, causal edge,
bounded policy, start, and release.

Workers must not promote their own dependencies without coordinator admission.

* Structured backpressure

Capacity exhaustion produces an =EvaluationCapacitySuspension= rather than an
unbounded queue, process hang, generic incompatibility, or hidden degradation.

The suspension records:

- demand and admission scope;
- unavailable resource dimensions;
- quota and current reservation evidence;
- queue saturation;
- deadline and freshness boundaries;
- causal propagation path;
- admitted recovery roles.

Recovery roles may include:

- wait with a bounded queue position;
- delegate to another admitted worker;
- release optional materialization;
- cancel the demand;
- form a new proposal with different resource requirements.

If recovery changes proof coverage, evaluation policy, approximation, or
semantic inputs, it creates a new semantic proposal. It must not mutate the
existing exact demand in place.

* Failure classification

The following remain distinct:

- contract contradiction is a semantic violation;
- missing evidence is an evidence suspension;
- capacity exhaustion is an admission suspension;
- deadline expiry is attempt termination evidence;
- worker loss is execution failure;
- stale reservation is a resource conflict;
- unresolved release is a reconciliation obligation.

Timeout and overload must not be reported as incompatibility.

* Preemption

Preemption is admitted only at a safe checkpoint boundary.

For a cyclic SCC:

- an internal node is not independently preempted without an admitted
  independence proof;
- the SCC owner defines safe joint checkpoint boundaries;
- reservation release is bound to a checkpoint receipt;
- unknown release enters reconciliation;
- the same resource is not reallocated until release is known or fenced.

Preemption without a portable checkpoint may cancel and restart a pure demand,
but it cannot claim suspended continuation recovery.

* Bounded queue and wake behavior

Every admission scope has bounded queue and checkpoint capacity.

- dynamic demand expansion reserves capacity before enqueue;
- suspension retains only a bounded logical checkpoint;
- raw continuations do not accumulate without a retention budget;
- watchers and negative-cache wake sets are coalesced;
- cancellation reaches children not yet admitted;
- repeated wake failure does not create unbounded retry work.

Backpressure propagates through causal demand edges to the owning parent.

* Starvation evidence

Fairness policy defines observable starvation conditions using admitted waiting
and service evidence.

A starvation observation may produce a scheduling adjustment proposal, bounded
urgency donation, capacity proposal, or operator evidence.

It must not silently change semantic priority or manufacture resource
authority.

* Observability receipts

The scheduler exposes evidence answering:

- why a demand was not admitted;
- which resource dimension blocked it;
- which scope owns the reservation;
- which fairness decision selected work;
- whether donation or preemption occurred;
- where the budget originated;
- how backpressure propagated;
- which condition may wake a suspension;
- whether starvation or reservation leakage is present.

Required receipts include:

- admission proposal and decision receipt;
- resource reservation and release receipt;
- fairness selection receipt;
- urgency donation receipt;
- backpressure suspension receipt;
- preemption and checkpoint receipt;
- resource reconciliation receipt.

* Failure taxonomy

The implementation must distinguish at least:

- semantic priority leaked into scheduling;
- resource snapshot incomplete;
- parent budget unavailable;
- child budget amplification attempted;
- reservation compare-and-swap conflict;
- queue capacity exhausted;
- fairness scope unresolved;
- unauthorized urgency donation;
- deadline expired;
- unsafe preemption boundary;
- checkpoint creation failed;
- reservation release unknown;
- wake storm coalesced;
- starvation observed;
- reservation leak suspected;
- backpressure receipt incomplete.

* Required invariants

1. Semantic contribution priority and runtime scheduling remain isolated.
2. Scheduling policy does not change canonical pure results.
3. Admission proposals hold no resource authority.
4. Resource reservations are hierarchical and conserved.
5. Child scopes cannot amplify parent budgets.
6. Fairness is scoped across tenants, roots, SCCs, and workload classes.
7. Urgency donation follows an exact causal edge and remains bounded.
8. Capacity exhaustion produces structured suspension.
9. Recovery that changes semantics creates a new proposal.
10. Timeout and overload are not compatibility violations.
11. Preemption requires a safe checkpoint boundary.
12. Queues, checkpoints, continuations, and wake sets remain bounded.
13. Unknown resource release enters reconciliation.
14. Scheduling decisions emit canonical observability receipts.
15. Public policy extension remains POO-native.
