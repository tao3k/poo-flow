#+title: Distributed Cache Admission, Provenance, and Poisoning Resistance
#+startup: overview

* Status

This RFC records the accepted admission boundary for cache reuse across
processes, hosts, and trust domains.

It extends incremental fixed-point cache correctness. Remote cache transport
does not replace local semantic, evidence, freshness, capability, or disclosure
admission.

* Typst formalization: local admission of distributed candidates

A distributed cache transports a candidate, never admission authority.  Local
use requires an independently produced receipt over the consumer's current
policy and observation state.

#+begin_src typst
#let local-checks(candidate, local) = (
  provenance-trusted(candidate.provenance, local.trust-policy)
  and candidate.semantic-key == local.expected-semantic-key
  and fresh(candidate, local.freshness-contract)
  and platform-compatible(candidate, local.platform)
  and confidentiality-allowed(candidate, local.domain)
  and not revoked(candidate.provenance, local.revocations)
  and determinism-consistent(candidate, local.known-results)
)

#let admits-local-use(candidate, local) = (
  local-checks(candidate, local)
  and local-receipt(candidate, local.policy-identity)
)

$ remote-candidate(c) ==> not local-authority(c) $

$ admits-local-use(c, l)
  ==> local-checks(c, l) and local-receipt(c, l.policy-identity) $
#+end_src

Poisoning resistance is fail-closed.  A semantic-key mismatch, stale evidence,
revocation, incompatible platform, confidentiality violation, or determinism
conflict prevents admission.  A determinism conflict additionally requires
quarantine so that the candidate cannot silently overwrite known evidence.

#+begin_src typst
#let mismatch(candidate, local) = (
  candidate.semantic-key != local.expected-semantic-key
  or not fresh(candidate, local.freshness-contract)
  or revoked(candidate.provenance, local.revocations)
  or not platform-compatible(candidate, local.platform)
  or not confidentiality-allowed(candidate, local.domain)
)

$ mismatch(c, l) ==> not admits-local-use(c, l) $

$ digest(c) != known-digest(c.semantic-key)
  ==> quarantine(c) and not admits-local-use(c, l) $

$ cache-class(c) = execution-work
  ==> not semantic-authority(c) $
#+end_src

* Central decision

A distributed cache transports candidate evidence and materializations.

It is not a local admission authority.

A remote cache hit grants no trust, disclosure right, action capability, or
proof status by itself.

Every consumer independently verifies and admits a candidate before local use.

* POO semantic ownership

Distributed cache admission policy is an ordinary higher-order POO object.
Native prototype composition defines:

- accepted cache classes;
- producer and provenance trust;
- semantic-key verification;
- freshness admission;
- platform and toolchain compatibility;
- confidentiality domains;
- revocation handling;
- determinism-violation response.

The public extension interface remains POO-native. Trust, cache class, or
confidentiality must not become an unstructured macro token DSL.

Remote cache implementation must not introduce a package registry or provider
registry as a second semantic authority.

* Distribution by cache class

The cache classes have different distribution boundaries.

** Pure semantic values

A pure semantic value may be reused across hosts when the consumer
independently verifies the complete semantic demand identity and dependency
manifest.

Platform independence must be declared and proven where relevant. It must not
be assumed from the value being serializable.

** Compatibility and evidence

A proof, violation, or suspension may be transported as immutable evidence.

The consumer readmits:

- the semantic closure input;
- dependency evidence;
- resource observations;
- compatibility policy;
- freshness;
- producer trust;
- disclosure scope.

A transported proof is not locally admitted until all these checks succeed.

** Execution work

Running evaluator attempts, waiter state, live budgets, cancellation scopes,
leases, and authority-bearing continuations are not ordinary distributed cache
entries.

An explicitly designed distributed evaluator may coordinate live work, but its
protocol belongs to execution authority rather than semantic cache reuse.

** Materializations

A materialization entry is scoped by:

- semantic materialization identity;
- source closure;
- build recipe;
- toolchain identity;
- target platform;
- ABI;
- dependency closure;
- resulting artifact digest.

A materialization hit does not prove semantic compatibility or activation
authority.

* Immutable remote descriptor

Every distributed candidate has an immutable descriptor containing:

- cache class;
- claimed semantic cache key;
- content digest;
- dependency manifest identity;
- producer identity;
- evaluator implementation identity;
- toolchain and platform identities where applicable;
- value, evidence, or materialization identity;
- confidentiality domain;
- creation observation;
- provenance or attestation chain;
- descriptor schema identity.

The claimed key is evidence supplied by the producer. It is not accepted
without local recomputation.

* Local admission

Local admission performs at least:

1. descriptor and content schema verification;
2. content digest verification;
3. producer provenance and trust admission;
4. independent semantic-key recomputation;
5. dependency-manifest validation;
6. graph, SCC, observation, and policy identity validation;
7. freshness readmission;
8. confidentiality and disclosure projection;
9. local cache-class admission;
10. admission receipt publication.

Failure at one phase must not be hidden by success at another phase.

* Independent semantic-key verification

For semantic and proof reuse, the consumer independently verifies at least:

- composition root and revision;
- demanded object and slot identities;
- composition strategy identity;
- dependency manifest identity;
- graph and SCC identities;
- observation-set identity;
- policy identity.

For materialization reuse, it additionally verifies:

- source closure;
- build recipe;
- toolchain;
- target platform;
- ABI;
- artifact digest.

The consumer must not trust a descriptor merely because it is signed. A valid
signature proves descriptor origin, not semantic correctness.

* Build-system boundary

Gerbil-Bazel, Nix, and other building systems may provide:

- build receipts;
- toolchain evidence;
- dependency closure evidence;
- artifact materializations;
- reproducibility evidence.

They remain evidence producers and building systems. Distributed cache
admission does not place them at the same semantic layer as POO composition.

Their receipts enter the relevant materialization and provenance checks.

* Determinism violations

The same complete semantic key must produce the same output identity.

If two admitted candidates claim the same complete key but produce different
output identities, the system must not use last-write-wins, newest-wins,
fastest-wins, or producer-priority selection.

It emits =DeterminismViolationEvidence= containing:

- semantic key identity;
- conflicting result identities;
- producer identities;
- dependency manifests;
- evaluator identities;
- provenance evidence;
- discovery and comparison receipts.

The affected namespace is isolated according to policy. The runtime performs
an authoritative local reevaluation or another explicitly admitted recovery
procedure.

Possible causes include:

- evaluator nondeterminism;
- incomplete dependency manifests;
- inconsistent producer implementations;
- forged descriptors;
- incorrect cache-key design.

The conflict remains observable even if a later reevaluation selects one
result.

* Distributed single-flight

Remote leases and work claims reduce duplicate computation. They are
performance coordination, not correctness authority.

Two hosts may legally compute the same pure demand concurrently.

Content-addressed compare-and-publish produces:

- natural convergence when result identities match;
- determinism-violation evidence when result identities differ.

Lease loss must not alter semantic results. A producer that loses its work
lease cannot publish authority-bearing live state through the cache.

* Confidentiality

Cache contents, keys, digests, and hit or miss observations may disclose
restricted information.

Admission policy may require:

- confidentiality-domain namespaces;
- domain-scoped discovery;
- encryption at rest and in transport;
- disclosure-aware receipts;
- domain-keyed opaque lookup identities;
- withheld or redacted evidence projections.

A result computed in a high-authority domain must not become visible to a
lower-authority domain through shared lookup, hit timing, descriptor access, or
evidence expansion.

Computation reuse does not grant disclosure.

* Revocation

Revocation is represented by a new immutable authoritative observation.

Revocation may cover:

- producer signing identity;
- evaluator implementation;
- artifact digest;
- vulnerable dependency;
- provenance authority;
- trust or disclosure policy.

An immutable cache entry and its historical receipts may remain available for
audit. Future admission fails when the current revocation observation rejects
the entry.

Deletion of every remote copy is not the security boundary.

Already admitted local proofs, activations, and materializations affected by a
new revocation enter reevaluation or policy-defined isolation. Revocation does
not silently rewrite their historical receipts.

* Remote misses

A remote miss normally means that the consumer evaluates or materializes
locally.

It is not a compatibility failure.

A miss becomes missing evidence only when policy requires an artifact or proof
that cannot be reconstructed in the admitted local environment.

Negative remote-cache responses must not be treated as authoritative proof
that an artifact does not exist elsewhere.

* Admission receipts

Every accepted or rejected candidate emits a local
=DistributedCacheAdmissionReceipt= containing:

- candidate descriptor identity;
- remote fetch source;
- content integrity result;
- provenance and trust-policy identities;
- recomputed semantic-key identity;
- dependency validation result;
- freshness admission result;
- confidentiality and disclosure projection;
- local acceptance or rejection reason;
- resulting local cache entry identity when accepted.

The receipt transfers evidence, not the producer's authority.

* Failure taxonomy

The implementation must distinguish at least:

- remote descriptor malformed;
- candidate content digest mismatch;
- producer provenance rejected;
- producer or artifact revoked;
- semantic-key recomputation mismatch;
- dependency manifest mismatch;
- graph or observation identity mismatch;
- proof freshness rejected;
- platform, toolchain, or ABI mismatch;
- confidentiality-domain violation;
- disclosure denied;
- determinism violation;
- remote lease lost;
- remote miss reconstructible;
- required materialization unavailable;
- admission receipt incomplete.

* Required invariants

1. A remote hit is only a candidate.
2. Local admission independently verifies semantic identity.
3. Cache classes retain distinct distribution boundaries.
4. Execution authority is not transferred through cache entries.
5. Signed provenance does not replace semantic verification.
6. Materializations are platform, toolchain, ABI, and closure scoped.
7. Same-key different-result conflicts fail closed.
8. Distributed single-flight remains a performance mechanism.
9. Computation reuse does not grant disclosure.
10. Cache keys and hit information obey confidentiality policy.
11. Revocation affects future admission without rewriting history.
12. Deletion is not the revocation security boundary.
13. Building systems remain evidence producers, not POO semantic peers.
14. Every remote admission or rejection emits a local receipt.
15. Public semantic extension remains POO-native.
