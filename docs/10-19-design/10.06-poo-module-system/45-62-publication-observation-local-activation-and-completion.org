#+title: Publication Observation, Local Activation, and Completion
#+startup: overview

* Status

This RFC records the accepted separation between active-head publication and
consumer activation.

It extends the governed rollout and multi-scope publication RFCs. It does not
redefine generation publication or multi-head atomicity.

* Central distinction

Active-head publication records the control-plane selection of an immutable
generation.

Local activation records that a particular consumer has safely adopted that
selection.

A successful head transition does not prove that any consumer has loaded,
activated, or served the selected generation.

The activation protocol has five distinct phases:

1. publication committed;
2. consumer observed;
3. generation prepared;
4. local activation;
5. rollout completion.

Each phase emits or consumes explicit evidence. They must not collapse into one
generic rollout-success state.

* POO semantic ownership

Activation and completion policy are ordinary higher-order POO objects. Native
prototype composition defines:

- required consumer scope;
- preparation contracts;
- safe activation boundaries;
- permitted drain overlap;
- completion coverage;
- activation deadlines;
- stale-consumer isolation proposals;
- continuation retention and migration policy.

The public extension interface must remain POO-native. Enum-like macro tokens
such as =#:all=, =#:quorum=, or =#:drain= must not become the semantic
extension mechanism.

Convenience syntax, if later admitted, may only project already defined POO
objects.

* Typst formalization: publication-to-completion evidence ladder

Publication changes the authoritative active head.  It does not establish that
any consumer has observed, prepared, activated, or served the selected
generation.  Each phase therefore carries a distinct receipt.

#+begin_src typst
$ P = HeadTransitionReceipt(scope, generation, version, receipt-id) $
$ O = HeadObservation(consumer, P, observed-generation, observation-receipt) $
$ G = GenerationPreparationReceipt(O, artifact-id, preparation-receipt) $
$ A = LocalActivationReceipt(G, activation-epoch, activation-receipt) $
$ C = RolloutCompletionReceipt(required-consumers, activations, completion-receipt) $

$ Published(P) not=> Observed(O) $
$ Published(P) not=> Prepared(G) $
$ Published(P) not=> LocallyActivated(A) $
$ Published(P) not=> Completed(C) $

$ P = ProjectAfter(ConditionalHeadCommit) $
$ CoupledPublication(P) => P in AtomicHeadSet_after $
#+end_src

The evidence ladder is monotone but not collapsible.  Local activation carries
the observation and preparation chain for the same generation.  Completion
requires activation evidence covering every consumer required by the rollout
completion policy.

#+begin_src typst
$ LocallyActivated(A)
  => exists G, O: A.preparation = G and G.observation = O $

$ Completed(C)
  => forall consumer in C.required-consumers:
    exists activation in C.activations:
      activation.consumer = consumer $

$ Observed(O) not=> LocallyActivated(A) $
$ Prepared(G) not=> LocallyActivated(A) $
$ LocallyActivated(A) not=> Completed(C) $
#+end_src

* Phase one: publication committed

A committed active-head transition emits an immutable
=HeadTransitionReceipt=.

The receipt proves:

- the publication scope;
- the previous and resulting head versions;
- the selected generation identity;
- the compare-and-swap result;
- the publication authority;
- the transition identity.

It does not prove consumer observation or activation.

* Phase two: consumer observed

Watcher delivery is a non-authoritative wake-up hint.

A consumer that receives a notification must read the authoritative active
head and construct a =HeadObservation= containing:

- consumer identity;
- publication scope;
- observed head version;
- selected generation identity;
- fencing information;
- authoritative source identity;
- observation identity.

Notifications may be lost, duplicated, delayed, or reordered without changing
the semantic result. Periodic reconciliation may discover a newer head even if
no notification was delivered.

A consumer accepts head versions monotonically within a scope. An older fenced
version must not reactivate an earlier generation.

* Desired state and transition obligations

A consumer may converge directly from an older observed head to the newest
authoritative head when intermediate transitions carry no mandatory migration
obligation.

If a transition contains a non-skippable migration obligation, that obligation
must be represented explicitly by policy and evidence. Notification order
must not be used as the migration protocol.

The desired active head and the append-only transition history remain separate
surfaces.

* Phase three: generation prepared

Before local activation, the consumer prepares an immutable activation
candidate by:

- acquiring the content-addressed generation artifact;
- verifying its digest and dependency closure;
- verifying required local capabilities;
- admitting resource and execution budgets;
- constructing the local runtime representation;
- recording the observed head and fencing versions.

Preparation failure emits a consumer-scoped =ActivationFailureReceipt=. It
does not mutate the active head and is not a publication failure.

Prepared artifacts may be cached by semantic identity. Live authority,
consumer identity, leases, and execution-local resources must not leak into
the semantic artifact cache key.

* Phase four: local activation

Activation occurs only at a safe flow boundary.

The default drain rule is:

- new flows bind to the newly activated generation;
- already running flows remain bound to their original generation;
- the earlier generation remains available until its admitted drain
  obligations are complete.

An =ActivationReceipt= records:

- consumer and publication scope identities;
- observed head and fencing versions;
- activated generation identity;
- previous local generation identity;
- activation boundary identity;
- runtime capability identity;
- permitted drain overlap;
- activation time and receipt identity.

* Controlled overlap and split-brain

The simultaneous presence of two generations is not automatically split-brain.

Controlled drain overlap is valid when:

- each flow is bound to exactly one generation;
- only new flows use the new generation;
- the overlap is admitted by activation policy;
- the old generation has a bounded drain obligation;
- both activation and drain are observable.

Invalid divergence exists when consumers accept incompatible heads for new
work beyond the admitted scope, fencing boundary, or convergence window.

The observability plane must distinguish controlled overlap from invalid
divergence.

* Continuation generation binding

A continuation carries a semantic dependency on the generation under which its
captured computation was admitted.

The runtime must associate the continuation with:

- semantic generation identity;
- flow identity;
- captured contract and capability projections;
- continuation creation evidence;
- permitted resumption policy.

Resuming a continuation after the active head changes uses the captured
generation by default.

Migration to a newer generation requires:

- a pure continuation migration proposal;
- compatibility evidence;
- newly admitted authority;
- a recoverable migration boundary;
- a migration or failure receipt.

The runtime must not silently rebind a continuation merely because a newer
active head exists.

* Phase five: rollout completion

A pure =ActivationCompletionPolicy= evaluates an immutable
=ActivationReceiptSet=.

The policy may compose:

- required consumer identity scope;
- consumer eligibility rules;
- exact or capability-weighted coverage predicates;
- permitted missing-consumer bounds;
- drain-completion requirements;
- activation deadlines;
- continuation retention obligations;
- freshness requirements for activation receipts.

The result is an =ActivationCompletionProposal=. The proposal is not authority
to isolate, restart, fence, or terminate a consumer.

A timeout produces incomplete or failed completion evidence. It must not be
interpreted as successful activation.

* Stale-consumer handling

The pure completion policy may propose that a stale or incompatible consumer
be isolated.

Isolation is a live authority action. It requires:

- a precise consumer target;
- stale-head or capability-failure evidence;
- an isolation capability;
- fencing or routing authority;
- an immutable action receipt.

Completion policy must not directly terminate or reroute a consumer.

* Failure taxonomy

The implementation must distinguish at least:

- watcher delivery failure;
- authoritative head observation failure;
- stale fenced head rejection;
- artifact acquisition failure;
- artifact digest or closure mismatch;
- missing local capability;
- preparation budget failure;
- unsafe activation boundary;
- local activation failure;
- drain deadline exceeded;
- continuation generation unavailable;
- continuation migration rejection;
- incomplete completion coverage;
- stale activation receipt;
- unauthorized consumer isolation.

* Required invariants

1. Publication success does not imply activation success.
2. Watcher notification is never authoritative evidence.
3. Consumers read and monotonically observe the authoritative head.
4. Old fenced versions cannot reactivate old generations.
5. Preparation completes before activation.
6. Activation occurs only at a safe flow boundary.
7. Every flow remains bound to exactly one semantic generation.
8. Controlled drain overlap is distinguished from split-brain.
9. Continuations retain their captured generation by default.
10. Continuation migration requires explicit evidence and authority.
11. Completion references an exact consumer scope and receipt set.
12. Timeout does not imply successful completion.
13. Isolation remains a live authorized action.
14. Public semantic extension remains POO-native.
