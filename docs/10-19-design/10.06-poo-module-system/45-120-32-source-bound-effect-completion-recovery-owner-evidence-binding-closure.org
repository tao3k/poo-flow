#+title: Source-bound effect completion recovery owner-evidence binding closure
#+startup: overview

* Status

This RFC closes the next v1 proof boundary after
[[file:45-120-31-source-bound-effect-completion-recovery-progress-evidence-closure.org][45-120-31]].

The previous RFC proves convergence from a concrete, owner-indexed decrease
receipt at every non-committed recovery step.  It intentionally leaves open
whether the current runtime, deterministic budget, provider, completion
storage, authority, freshness, and Cedar-policy evidence can construct those
receipts.

This continuation binds the evidence that already has an owner and proves the
remaining non-entailments.  It does not invent a crash counter, reinterpret a
semantic budget as storage admission, or let policy evidence manufacture
runtime progress.

* Lean-discovered gap: existing evidence does not construct liveness

The current system already has:

- deterministic scheduling and semantic budget receipts;
- provider recovery observations bound to provider identity, recovery step,
  runtime epoch, and fence;
- atomic completion commit receipts;
- Cedar dual-engine decision receipts;
- accountable human authority grants and receipts;
- evidence-freshness checks.

These are necessary safety and authorization gates.  They do not jointly imply
the three-coordinate progress receipt introduced by 45-120-31.

The missing implication would be:

#+begin_src typst
$ op("CurrentOwnerEvidence")(i)
  => exists o,
    op("ProgressReceipt")(B_i, B_(i + 1), o) $
#+end_src

Lean rejects that implication for three independent reasons:

1. the deterministic receipt exposes no crash-deferral coordinate;
2. its semantic budget has no declared equality with completion-storage
   deferral;
3. provider, authorization, freshness, and commit evidence do not prove a
   strict decrease of any progress coordinate.

* Decision

Bind current evidence in three separate proof surfaces:

1. an authorized owner assertion binds one complete Cedar authorization
   subject across both Cedar engines, the human grant, the accepted authority
   receipt, the approval capability, freshness checks, and the recovery epoch;
2. a deterministic scheduling binding projects only
   =schedulingRemaining= into =remainingSchedulingDeferrals=;
3. existing provider observation and completion commit closures retain their
   current safety roles and are never promoted into progress receipts.

The convergence theorem continues to require the concrete progress evidence
from 45-120-31:

#+begin_src typst
$ op("TransitionClosed")(tau)
  and op("ProgressEvidence")(tau, B)
  and op("CurrentOwnerGates")
  => op("Converges")(tau) $
#+end_src

The proof reuses the existing convergence theorem.  The owner gates are
orthogonal premises, not an alternative liveness proof.

* Complete authorization-subject binding

=BoundAuthorityReceipt.subject= and =AuthorityGrant.scopeSubject= already use
the complete =AuthorizationSubject= type.  The binding therefore uses exact
subject equality rather than reducing the subject to a request digest.

For one canonical subject =u=, the authorized assertion requires:

#+begin_src typst
$ u_L = u_R = u_G = u_A = u
  and op("epoch")(u) = e
  and c_G = c_A = "approve" $
#+end_src

where:

- =u_L= and =u_R= are the left and right Cedar receipt subjects;
- =u_G= is the authority-grant scope subject;
- =u_A= is the accepted authority-receipt subject;
- =e= is the recovery epoch;
- =c_G= and =c_A= are the grant and receipt capabilities.

This preserves the full request, bundle, policy-set, entity-store, and epoch
identity carried by =AuthorizationSubject=.

* Cedar agreement is not a permit

=dualDecisionEvidenceClosed= proves that the two independent engines agree,
their receipts are distinct and valid, and both decisions are semantically
correct.  Agreement can still be an agreed denial.

The authorized assertion therefore adds the explicit permit condition:

#+begin_src typst
$ d_L = "allow"
  and d_L = d_R
  => d_R = "allow" $
#+end_src

A closed dual-engine decision whose left receipt is =deny= cannot inhabit the
authorized owner assertion.

This is not a third policy engine.  It is the required interpretation of the
existing Cedar decision evidence at the recovery-owner boundary.

* Freshness is not subject identity

=EvidenceFreshness.Hold= proves:

- exact evidence cut;
- validity interval;
- current revocation observation;
- not revoked;
- current policy revision;
- current source revision.

The =Checks= value carries no authorization subject.  The same fresh checks can
therefore coexist with two distinct subjects:

#+begin_src typst
$ op("Hold")(F)
  => exists u',
    op("Hold")(F)
    and u' != u $
#+end_src

The Lean witness keeps every subject field unchanged except:

#+begin_src typst
$ op("epoch")(u') = op("epoch")(u) + 1 $
#+end_src

Consequently freshness must be combined with explicit subject and epoch
equalities.  =Hold= alone cannot bind a recovery authorization.

* Deterministic scheduling projection

The existing deterministic budget receipt has =schedulingRemaining= and
=semanticRemaining=.  Only the scheduling coordinate has a declared recovery
projection:

#+begin_src typst
$ s(B) = op("schedulingRemaining")(R_D) $
#+end_src

The binding additionally requires the existing
=SourceBoundDeterministicBudgetEvidenceClosed= proposition.  It does not create
a second deterministic-budget validator.

The projection leaves the crash coordinate underdetermined.  For the same
receipt =R_D=:

#+begin_src typst
$ B_0 = (0, s(R_D), 0)
  and B_1 = (1, s(R_D), 0)
  and rho(B_0) != rho(B_1) $
#+end_src

It also leaves the completion-storage coordinate underdetermined:

#+begin_src typst
$ B_0 = (0, s(R_D), 0)
  and B_1 = (0, s(R_D), 1)
  and rho(B_0) != rho(B_1) $
#+end_src

No theorem identifies =semanticRemaining= with storage deferral.  Such an
identity would require an explicit storage-owner contract and cannot be
inferred from similar terminology.

* Provider evidence is a scope gate

A closed provider recovery observation binds the expected recovery identity,
provider identity, step, runtime epoch, active fence, and any acknowledged
completion receipt.

Even with that closure, an unchanged progress budget has no owner receipt:

#+begin_src typst
$ op("ProviderObservationClosed")(O)
  => not exists o,
    op("ProgressReceipt")(B, B, o) $
#+end_src

This preserves the provider's current role:

- acknowledgement constrains the authoritative recovery world;
- acknowledgement does not schedule work;
- acknowledgement does not force storage acceptance;
- acknowledgement does not decrease a proof budget.

* Authorization cannot manufacture progress

The same separation applies to a fully authorized owner assertion:

#+begin_src typst
$ op("AuthorizedOwnerAssertion")(A)
  => not exists o,
    op("ProgressReceipt")(B, B, o) $
#+end_src

Policy and accountability answer who may assert or approve an owner bound.
They do not prove that a runtime crash, scheduler deferral, or storage deferral
was consumed.

* Commit evidence is post-commit evidence

The existing completion commit receipt proves the atomic publication boundary
after commit.  It has no pre-commit storage-deferral coordinate.

For the same commit receipt, Lean constructs two compatible proof budgets with
different storage components:

#+begin_src typst
$ exists B_0, B_1,
  a(B_0) != a(B_1)
  and rho(B_0) != rho(B_1) $
#+end_src

The theorem does not weaken commit atomicity.  It prevents a post-commit receipt
from being misused as a bounded pre-commit admission contract.

* Composition theorem

=closedOwnerGatesPreserveExistingConvergence= accepts:

- the existing safe recovery transition closure;
- the concrete progress evidence from 45-120-31;
- a closed deterministic scheduling binding;
- a closed provider observation;
- a closed authorized owner assertion.

It concludes convergence only by applying =closedProgressEvidenceConverges=:

#+begin_src typst
$ T(tau)
  and P(tau, B)
  and D
  and O
  and A
  => C(tau) $
#+end_src

The theorem deliberately cannot construct =P= from =D=, =O=, and =A=.  The two
underdetermination witnesses prove why that construction is unavailable.

* Enterprise consequence

Enterprise recovery requires both kinds of assurance:

1. authorization assurance: the actor, capability, policy decision, evidence
   cut, subject, and epoch are exact and accountable;
2. progress assurance: an existing runtime owner proves a bounded decrease for
   every non-committed step.

Neither assurance subsumes the other.  A permitted action may stall forever,
and a mechanically decreasing action may still be unauthorized.  Enterprise
closure requires their conjunction while preserving their owners.

* Proof findings

Lean exposes and closes these v1 design defects:

1. Complete authority-subject equality is available and must not be reduced to
   a digest-only binding.
2. Dual Cedar agreement does not imply permit; =allow= is an explicit premise.
3. Freshness checks carry no subject identity and cannot bind an authorization
   by themselves.
4. The deterministic receipt projects the scheduling coordinate only.
5. The scheduling projection leaves both crash and storage budgets
   underdetermined.
6. =semanticRemaining= is not a declared completion-storage deferral budget.
7. Closed provider evidence cannot justify an unchanged progress step.
8. Closed authorization evidence cannot justify an unchanged progress step.
9. A completion commit receipt does not determine a pre-commit storage bound.
10. Existing owner gates compose with convergence only while concrete progress
    evidence remains an explicit premise.

* Next proof gap

The owner binding is now exact where current evidence exists.  Two bounded
liveness obligations remain genuinely absent:

- a runtime-owned finite crash-deferral contract;
- a completion-storage-owned bounded pre-commit admission contract.

The next proof must determine whether current runtime and storage owners already
have suitable evidence under another name.  If they do not, the architecture
must discuss the smallest owner-native receipt contract before any
implementation is added.

The existing =semanticRemaining= coordinate must not be reused for storage
without that discussion and a proved semantic equivalence.
