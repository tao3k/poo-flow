#+title: Deterministic Parallel POO Evaluation and Canonical Commit
#+status: Accepted
#+language: en

* Decision

Parallel execution may change when a POO demand completes.  It MUST NOT change:

- the composed POO value;
- prototype precedence;
- slot-contract combination;
- priority selection or ambiguity;
- capability and authority decisions;
- fixed-point convergence;
- the canonical condition set;
- the canonical semantic receipt.

Workers produce pure candidate observations.  A canonical semantic commit
seals the current generation's value, dependency topology, conditions and
evidence.

The commit is a logical evidence boundary.  It is not a second evaluator, a
mutable global configuration store or a realization side effect.

* Native POO boundary

=gerbil-poo= remains the sole implementation of objects, prototypes, slot
demand and open recursion.  POO Flow schedules native demand and observes its
results.

The scheduler MUST NOT:

- replace native slot lookup or computation;
- turn worker completion order into prototype precedence;
- introduce a public task-graph DSL;
- expose cache, worker or queue configuration as ordinary composition syntax;
- make raw futures, promises or thread handles into module values;
- commit effects from the pure control plane.

Public module semantics remain POO-native.  Operational scheduling policy may
itself be represented by an authorized POO policy object, but it cannot change
the meaning of composition.

* Logical evaluation frontier

A logical frontier is the canonical set of slot demands or sealed SCCs that are
eligible to proceed under the current generation and dependency evidence.

Members of the same frontier may finish in any physical order.  Their candidate
observations are not semantically visible merely because they finished.

* Parallelization boundaries

** Acyclic demands

Two acyclic slot demands may execute concurrently when the current dependency
evidence does not place either demand downstream of the other.

A newly discovered dependency may invalidate this provisional independence.
The canonical commit then rejects or revises the affected candidate rather
than publishing an order-dependent result.

** Independent SCCs

Two sealed SCCs may execute concurrently when neither SCC depends on the other
and their authority, budget and realization boundaries permit the work.

Each SCC remains an atomic convergence and receipt unit.

** Within an SCC

An SCC is not internally parallel by default.

Parallel member evaluation is permitted only when the governing POO
slot-contract objects establish the laws needed for schedule independence.
Depending on the contract, these may include:

- associativity;
- commutativity;
- idempotence;
- monotonicity;
- a stable base;
- deterministic convergence;
- a canonical representation of equal results.

If those laws are absent, POO Flow retains the native deterministic evaluation
route for the SCC.  Performance pressure does not authorize a semantic race.

* Native precedence is not canonical sorting

Canonicalization applies to unordered evidence, candidate sets and diagnostic
projection.  It MUST NOT reorder a semantic operation whose contract is
ordered.

In particular:

- =.mix= and native prototype lookup retain their declared precedence;
- ordered same-lineage refinement retains its POO order;
- authority attenuation retains its causal delegation order;
- receipt predecessor chains retain causal order;
- unordered contribution sets may use a canonical identity order only for
  representation and comparison.

Canonical ordering is therefore a serialization and evidence property, not a
replacement merge algebra.

* No race-first-wins semantics

The following decisions require the complete causally relevant candidate
closure and MUST NOT be decided by the first worker or cache response:

- singleton capability selection;
- merge-priority selection;
- equal-priority conflict;
- contract validation;
- force-authority validation;
- capability ambiguity;
- fixed-point convergence;
- SCC topology sealing;
- continuable condition selection.

* Canonical semantic commit

A candidate observation supplied to the commit includes:

- generation-bound slot-demand identity;
- candidate result identity;
- newly observed dependency edges;
- contribution and contract obligations;
- priority, capability and authority evidence;
- SCC membership or topology revision evidence;
- continuable conditions;
- logical budget work consumed;
- predecessor receipts used for reuse.

The commit:

1. rejects stale-generation candidates;
2. combines newly observed edges into a canonical set;
3. determines whether the current SCC topology remains valid;
4. revokes provisional convergence invalidated by a topology change;
5. applies the native ordered or unordered slot-contract algebra;
6. constructs the canonical condition closure;
7. seals the value and semantic receipt only after all obligations succeed.

The commit does not perform external effects.  Realization remains behind the
explicit effect boundary.

* Topology changes during parallel work

Concurrent work may discover an edge that grows, merges, splits or dissolves an
SCC.  A worker result computed against the previous provisional topology is not
automatically valid.

#+begin_src mermaid
flowchart LR
  P["Parallel candidate observations"] -->|"discover a new cross-component edge"| N["Topology revision"]
  N -->|"invalidates affected provisional convergence"| X["Candidate revocation"]
  X -->|"expands or repartitions the recomputation closure"| S["New SCC frontier"]
  S -->|"reruns only the causally affected work"| P
  S -->|"stabilizes membership and edges"| C["Canonical commit"]
  C -->|"records the triggering edge and revision history"| R["Current-generation receipt"]
#+end_src

Revocation here is pure semantic invalidation.  It is not destructive rollback
of an external effect, because effects were not committed by workers.

* Deterministic condition aggregation

The first condition to arrive in wall-clock time is not the semantic condition.

Conditions discovered in the same logical frontier are accumulated and
canonically projected by stable semantic dimensions such as:

1. contribution generation;
2. SCC identity;
3. POO object identity;
4. slot identity;
5. causal demand path;
6. condition identity.

The interface may present one condition as primary for interaction, but every
condition in the relevant frontier remains in the evidence closure.

A primary condition is therefore a deterministic projection, not the winner of
a runtime race.

* Logical and operational budgets

Semantic convergence must use reproducible logical work measurements.

| Budget class | Examples | Semantic role |
|--------------+----------+---------------|
| Logical | demand count, dependency-edge observations, SCC topology revisions, convergence steps, resumption generations | May establish bounded convergence or a reproducible continuable condition |
| Operational | wall time, worker count, CPU pressure, queue latency, cache latency, memory pressure | May admit, suspend or cancel a run but cannot prove semantic non-convergence |

An operational timeout may stop the current execution and produce an
operational failure receipt.  It MUST NOT be reported as proof that the POO
fixed point is semantically divergent.

* Semantic and operational receipts

Two evidence surfaces are required.

** Canonical semantic receipt

The semantic receipt commits to:

- generation and POO identities;
- complete contribution closure;
- native slot-contract result;
- dependency and sealed SCC identities;
- canonical condition closure;
- logical budgets;
- authority and capability decisions;
- predecessor reuse and replay equivalence.

Equivalent evaluations under different valid schedules MUST produce the same
canonical semantic receipt.

** Operational receipt

The operational receipt may include:

- worker identities;
- scheduling and work-stealing events;
- physical completion order;
- wall-clock durations;
- queue and host pressure;
- cache hit, miss and retrieval latency;
- cancellation or admission decisions.

Operational receipts are causally linked to the semantic receipt but do not
participate in its content identity.

* Cache timing independence

A verified cache result and a freshly computed result are interchangeable only
after replay-equivalence validation.

- Cache arrival order cannot select a value.
- A cache miss cannot change the canonical semantic receipt.
- A cache hit is recorded in the operational receipt.
- Semantic reuse and its predecessor evidence are recorded in the semantic
  receipt.
- A cache result rejected for stale identity contributes operational evidence
  but not a semantic candidate.

The same POO result reached through valid cache reuse or fresh evaluation must
have the same semantic identity.

* Cancellation and recovery

External cancellation is an operational lifecycle event.  It may prevent a
semantic commit but cannot manufacture a partial semantic result.

When a logical condition offers an authorized continuation:

- the condition closure is canonical;
- the chosen recovery action is recorded causally;
- resumption enters a new contribution generation;
- unfinished worker candidates from the predecessor generation are stale;
- scheduling may change without changing the successor generation's semantics.

* Required failure conditions

Evaluation MUST fail or enter an authorized continuable condition when:

- worker completion order changes a semantic decision;
- an incomplete candidate closure selects a singleton or priority winner;
- unordered canonical sorting is applied to ordered POO precedence;
- an SCC is evaluated internally in parallel without the required algebraic
  laws;
- a topology revision is ignored because a worker already completed;
- the first wall-clock condition replaces deterministic condition aggregation;
- an operational timeout is presented as semantic divergence;
- cache arrival order changes the canonical receipt;
- an external effect occurs before semantic commit and cannot be safely
  reconciled.

* Acceptance checklist

- [X] Scheduler order is non-semantic.
- [X] Independent acyclic demands and independent SCCs may execute in
  parallel.
- [X] An SCC is internally serial unless its POO slot contracts establish safe
  parallel laws.
- [X] Native ordered prototype precedence is never replaced by canonical
  sorting.
- [X] Singleton, priority, authority, ambiguity and convergence decisions
  reject race-first-wins behavior.
- [X] Workers submit pure candidate observations.
- [X] Canonical semantic commit seals values, topology, conditions and
  receipts.
- [X] Topology revisions revoke affected provisional candidates.
- [X] Logical and operational budgets are distinct.
- [X] Canonical semantic and operational receipts are distinct.
- [X] Cache timing cannot change semantic identity.
- [X] External effects remain outside worker and semantic-commit execution.
- [ ] Validate schedule independence with executable worker-order, cache-order,
  SCC-revision, multi-condition, timeout and resumption scenarios before
  implementation begins.
