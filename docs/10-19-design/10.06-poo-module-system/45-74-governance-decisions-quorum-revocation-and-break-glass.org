#+title: Governance Decisions, Quorum, Revocation, and Break-Glass
#+startup: overview

* Status

This RFC records the accepted governance intervention semantics for human and
institutional decisions in POO Flow.

It extends proposal, evidence, capability, continuation, effect, and audit
contracts. A governance decision is immutable evidence. It is not a permanent
or general-purpose action capability.

* Typst formalization: bound governance evidence and scoped capability

Governance approval is evidence, not action authority.  A decision may request
a capability only when every binding check still holds for the exact proposal,
evidence cut, quorum, separation of duties, scope, target version, policy, and
time observation.

#+begin_src typst
#let governance-checks(decision, current) = (
  decision.proposal == current.proposal
  and decision.evidence-cut == current.evidence-cut
  and quorum-satisfied(decision)
  and separation-satisfied(decision)
  and decision.scope == current.required-scope
  and cas-precondition-holds(decision.target-version)
  and decision.policy == current.policy
  and not expired(decision, current.time-observation)
  and not revoked(decision)
)

$ governance-approval(decision)
  ==> evidence(decision)
  and not action-authority(decision) $

$ issue-capability(decision)
  ==> governance-checks(decision, current)
  and scoped(decision.capability)
  and one-shot(decision.capability) $
#+end_src

Any change to proposal, evidence cut, scope, or policy creates a new governance
decision identity.  Break-glass remains an explicit policy path with narrow
scope, expiry, reason, receipt, and mandatory post-action review; it never
creates global approval state.

#+begin_src typst
#let decision-id(proposal, cut, scope, policy) = (
  proposal,
  cut,
  scope,
  policy,
)

$ proposal-a != proposal-b
  ==> decision-id(proposal-a, cut, scope, policy)
  != decision-id(proposal-b, cut, scope, policy) $

$ cut-a != cut-b or scope-a != scope-b or policy-a != policy-b
  ==> decision-id(proposal, cut-a, scope-a, policy-a)
  != decision-id(proposal, cut-b, scope-b, policy-b) $

$ break-glass(decision)
  ==> policy-allows-break-glass(decision.policy)
  and explicit(decision.scope)
  and explicit(decision.expiry)
  and recorded(decision.reason)
  and required(decision.post-action-review)
  and not global-authority(decision) $

$ revoked(decision) ==> not issue-capability(decision) $
#+end_src

* Central decision

Human approval is governance evidence.

Only when the exact proposal, evidence cut, quorum, separation, scope, version,
and temporal requirements remain satisfied may an authority service issue a
one-shot scoped action capability.

There is no unbounded system-level state meaning that an administrator approved
the system in general.

* POO semantic ownership

Governance policy is represented by ordinary higher-order POO objects. Native
prototype composition defines:

- decision roles;
- quorum requirements;
- separation of duties;
- independence domains;
- delegation;
- evidence disclosure;
- expiry and revocation;
- capability issuance;
- emergency break-glass;
- post-action obligations.

The public governance interface may be concise, but it must expand or compile
to ordinary POO governance objects, evidence contracts, and capability
requests.

Macros must not hide quorum, scope, expiry, evidence binding, or authority.

* Governance decision binding

Every immutable governance decision binds:

- exact proposal identity;
- semantic generation or composition identity;
- evidence-cut identity;
- target resource and scope;
- requested action role;
- version and compare-and-swap preconditions;
- risk classification;
- governance policy identity;
- principal identity;
- principal role and capability evidence;
- delegation chain;
- decision time observation and expiry;
- constraints and reason;
- disclosure projection identity;
- decision receipt identity.

A change to proposal content, target version, evidence cut, policy, or required
scope creates a new governance decision boundary.

Old decisions do not transfer automatically to the new proposal.

* Decision roles

Governance decisions use explicit POO evidence roles such as:

- approval evidence;
- rejection evidence;
- abstention or no-decision evidence;
- request-for-more-evidence;
- revocation evidence.

These roles are not arbitrary UI strings.

A missing decision is not approval. An abstention is not rejection. A request
for additional evidence leaves authorization suspended.

* Quorum

Quorum is not a count of signatures alone.

A pure quorum policy verifies:

- distinct principals;
- required role coverage;
- independence domains;
- proposer and approver separation;
- operator and auditor separation;
- permitted delegation;
- delegation-root independence;
- role validity at decision time;
- sufficient evidence disclosure for each decision;
- binding to the same proposal revision.

Two signatures derived from the same root delegation may count as one
independent authority source when policy requires independent authority.

The quorum proof identifies every admitted principal, role, delegation path,
independence domain, and decision.

* Identity, authentication, and authorization

Principal authentication proves who produced a decision.

Role and delegation evidence proves what governance role the principal held at
the decision time.

Quorum policy proves that the decision set satisfies governance requirements.

Capability issuance authorizes one exact live action.

These proofs remain separate. A valid signature does not provide action
authority by itself.

* Evidence disclosure

A decision records the exact evidence projection available to its principal.

Policy verifies that the projection is sufficient for the required decision
role.

If required evidence is hidden, stale, or redacted beyond the policy contract,
the result is request-for-more-evidence or suspension.

A principal must not approve a high-confidence claim based on an evidence
projection that proves only a weaker claim.

Confidential evidence remains disclosure-scoped in decisions and audit
receipts.

* Capability issuance

After pure governance authorization, an authority service may issue a
capability bound to:

- exact action role;
- exact resource scope;
- exact proposal identity;
- expected resource or head version;
- decision-set and quorum-proof identities;
- expiry and time authority;
- transfer policy;
- one-shot or affine consumption policy;
- capability issuance identity.

The capability is short-lived and non-transferable by default.

Consumption emits a receipt. Expired or consumed capability cannot be restored
by replaying approval evidence.

Approval replay explains historical issuance. It does not reacquire live
authority.

* Revocation before action

A new authoritative revocation observation may invalidate an unconsumed
decision, quorum proof, or issued capability.

Action admission rechecks:

- proposal revision;
- governance decision set;
- revocation set;
- capability expiry;
- target resource version;
- fencing and compare-and-swap preconditions.

Revocation and action consumption use authoritative logical versioning and
compare-and-swap to determine ordering.

Wall-clock timestamps alone must not decide a concurrent revocation race.

* Revocation after action

Revocation does not erase an action that already committed.

Policy may produce a new:

- reversion proposal;
- compensation proposal;
- isolation proposal;
- incident obligation;
- governance review obligation.

Every response requires its own evidence, authority, and receipts.

Historical decision, authorization, capability-consumption, and action receipts
remain immutable.

* Emergency break-glass

Break-glass is a constrained emergency governance policy. It is not unrestricted
root authority.

An emergency decision identifies:

- incident identity;
- exact action and resource scope;
- short expiry and lease;
- explicit reason;
- emergency principal and role;
- witness or independent approval when required;
- policy clauses that may be relaxed;
- invariants that remain mandatory;
- post-action review and remediation obligations.

Break-glass must not bypass:

- semantic and content identity;
- resource ownership;
- compare-and-swap and fencing;
- destructive target precision;
- immutable receipts;
- unknown-outcome reconciliation.

It may relax only the approval requirements explicitly declared relaxable by
the governance policy.

Every successful, failed, expired, and unused emergency capability produces
evidence.

* Continuable governance intervention

A suspended evaluator may expose constrained restart roles.

A governance decision selecting a restart binds:

- suspension identity;
- exact restart role;
- current semantic and evidence cut;
- continuation identity;
- required capability;
- expected resource versions;
- expiry.

If the cut, continuation, proposal, or resource version changes, the prior
human decision cannot resume the new execution.

Selecting a restart does not authorize unrelated actions.

* Three evidence chains

A completed governance action closes three separate chains.

** Decision chain

The decision chain proves:

- who decided;
- under which role and delegation;
- from which evidence projection;
- for which exact proposal;
- at which admitted time;
- with which constraints.

** Authorization chain

The authorization chain proves:

- which governance policy applied;
- how quorum and separation were satisfied;
- which revocations were checked;
- why capability issuance was admitted;
- which exact capability was issued.

** Action chain

The action chain proves:

- how the capability was consumed;
- which resource versions were observed;
- what action was attempted;
- whether the outcome succeeded, failed, or remained unknown;
- how reconciliation closed the outcome.

An approval signature, UI =Approved= label, or successful operator command
alone does not close all three chains.

* Failure taxonomy

The implementation must distinguish at least:

- proposal decision binding mismatch;
- principal authentication rejected;
- role evidence invalid;
- delegation chain invalid;
- independence domain insufficient;
- quorum incomplete;
- separation of duties violated;
- evidence projection insufficient;
- decision expired;
- decision revoked;
- capability issuance rejected;
- capability expired or consumed;
- target version changed;
- revocation race unresolved;
- break-glass scope exceeded;
- mandatory invariant bypass attempted;
- governance restart stale;
- action outcome unknown;
- governance audit closure incomplete.

* Required invariants

1. Governance decisions are immutable evidence, not permanent capabilities.
2. Every decision binds one exact proposal and evidence cut.
3. Quorum verifies roles, principals, delegation, and independence.
4. Authentication, quorum authorization, and live capability remain separate.
5. Evidence disclosure must satisfy the decision contract.
6. Issued capabilities are exact, short-lived, and affine or one-shot.
7. Replay does not reacquire capability.
8. Pre-action revocation is checked at action admission.
9. Concurrent revocation ordering uses authoritative versions.
10. Post-action revocation does not erase committed actions.
11. Break-glass remains narrow and preserves mandatory invariants.
12. Human restart selection binds exact suspension and continuation identities.
13. Decision, authorization, and action chains close separately.
14. Public governance extension remains POO-native.
