#+title: Demand Admission, Backpressure, And Cleanup Capacity

* Normative responsibility

This module owns POO Flow policy and evidence semantics for:

- admitting demanded realization work to a runtime;
- suspending work under backpressure;
- allocating hierarchical boundary budgets;
- reserving bounded cleanup, release, and reconciliation capacity;
- accounting for coalesced provider attempts;
- observing starvation and capacity uncertainty.

It does not implement a scheduler.  Runtime or Marlin providers own execution
queues, threads, processes, event loops, and host-specific scheduling.

* Ownership boundary: gerbil-poo mechanism and POO Flow governance

=gerbil-poo= owns lazy demand, memoization, fixed-point evaluation, suspension,
lineage, and continuation-aware control.

POO Flow owns:

- admission classes and policy;
- boundary budget identity and containment allocation;
- capacity-permit evidence;
- backpressure and starvation conditions;
- cleanup-capacity reservation;
- admission, defer, transfer, return, and escalation receipts.

[[file:45-09-effect-intent-suspension-handoff-reinjection.org][Effect Intent, Suspension, Runtime Handoff, And Receipt Reinjection]] owns
effect suspension and coalesced attempts.  [[file:45-12-structured-cancellation-boundary-generations.org][Structured Cancellation And Boundary Generations]]
owns ordinary versus cleanup continuation authority.
[[file:45-11-resource-lease-continuation-dynamic-wind.org][Resource Lease, Continuation, And Dynamic-Wind]] owns resource release proof.

* Admission is not scheduling

Admission answers whether a demanded unit of work has policy, budget, and
provider capacity authority to execute.  Scheduling answers when and where an
admitted unit runs.

POO Flow defines the first question and records its evidence.  It MUST NOT:

- implement a private task scheduler;
- select operating-system threads or processes;
- busy-poll a provider queue;
- infer host capacity without provider evidence;
- change POO refinement precedence to influence execution order.

An admitted demand remains subject to runtime scheduling.  Admission is not a
claim that execution has already begun.

* Admission classes

Every admission candidate has exactly one policy class:

| Class | Purpose | Authority source |
|---+---+---|
| =ordinary= | pure demanded realization and ordinary application progress | active ordinary boundary generation |
| =effect= | authorized provider invocation or external operation progress | effect-intent and provider handoff receipts |
| =cleanup= | cancellation propagation, release, reconciliation, and terminal receipt construction | restricted cleanup generation |

Class is internal policy evidence, not a Profile case, public runtime type, or
user-authored queue label.

After a boundary leaves =active=, it cannot create new =ordinary= or unrelated
=effect= admissions.  Its restricted generation may admit only =cleanup= work
within the authority defined by 45-12.

* Admission pipeline

#+begin_src mermaid
flowchart TD
  D["State: demanded POO realization"] -->|"dependency evidence is ready"| A["State: admission candidate"]
  A -->|"policy + boundary budget + provider capacity permit"| R["State: runtime-admitted demand"]
  R -->|"one execution or coalesced provider attempt"| X["State: active realization"]
  X -->|"terminal receipt returns capacity"| T["State: completed demand"]
  A -->|"capacity unavailable"| B["State: suspended backpressure condition"]
  B -->|"capacity receipt becomes available"| A
  B -->|"policy-authorized defer"| B
  B -->|"deadline or budget expiry requests cancellation"| C["State: cancellation requested"]
  K["State: cleanup, release, or reconciliation demand"] -->|"reserved bounded cleanup authority"| Q["State: cleanup admission lane"]
  Q -->|"provider capacity permit"| X
  K -.->|"cleanup must not compete solely with ordinary work"| A
#+end_src

Lazy graph traversal does not create an admission candidate.  A candidate exists
only when ordinary =gerbil-poo= realization actually demands the value or when
restricted cleanup authority demands governed terminal work.

* Capacity permit identity

A capacity permit is context-bound authority.  Its evidence identifies:

- runtime provider and capacity domain;
- realization context and structural boundary;
- admission class;
- budget allocation;
- admitted demand or coalesced attempt;
- acquisition, use, return, and predecessor receipts.

It is not a portable value, POO capability identity, package dependency,
runtime handle, or lexical binding name.  It MUST NOT be serialized or reused
across realization contexts.

A provider that cannot prove available capacity cannot issue a permit.  POO
Flow fails closed by retaining the demand under backpressure or raising a
structured capacity-unobservable condition according to policy.

* Hierarchical boundary budgets

Budget ownership follows structural containment:

#+begin_src mermaid
flowchart TD
  P["State: parent boundary capacity policy"] -->|"explicit containment allocation receipt"| C1["State: child budget"]
  P -->|"explicit containment allocation receipt"| C2["State: child budget"]
  C1 -->|"unused capacity returns by policy receipt"| P
  C2 -->|"unused capacity returns by policy receipt"| P
  I["State: imported Mix dependency"] -.->|"dependency does not transfer budget ownership"| P
  D1["State: joined demand A"] -->|"shares one admitted attempt"| O["State: coalesced operation"]
  D2["State: joined demand B"] -->|"shares one admitted attempt"| O
#+end_src

A child receives capacity only through an explicit containment allocation.
Unused allocation may return to its parent only through an immutable policy
receipt.

Mix =imports= do not transfer or duplicate budget ownership.  An imported
provider may report capacity in its own domain, but that dependency edge does
not grant the importing boundary authority to spend it.

The same local budget name under different parents does not imply the same
budget identity.

* Reserved cleanup capacity

Cleanup must remain possible when ordinary work saturates its capacity domain.
Each runtime capacity policy therefore provides a bounded cleanup reserve or a
provider-backed proof that cleanup can preempt ordinary admission safely.

Ordinary work MUST NOT consume the final cleanup authority merely because the
cleanup lane is idle.  A provider may lend reserved capacity only when it proves
bounded, immediate revocation before cleanup demand can be starved.

#+begin_src mermaid
flowchart LR
  C["State: total provider capacity evidence"] -->|"ordinary allocation"| O["State: bounded ordinary capacity"]
  C -->|"non-starvable reservation"| K["State: bounded cleanup capacity"]
  O -->|"ordinary terminal receipt"| C
  K -->|"cleanup terminal receipt"| C
  O -.->|"cannot consume final cleanup authority without revocation proof"| K
  X["State: boundary quiescing"] -->|"restricted cleanup generation"| K
  K -->|"release, cancel, reconcile, or build terminal receipt"| X
#+end_src

Cleanup capacity is not unlimited.  Exhaustion follows explicit cleanup budget,
deadline, and escalation policy.  If bounded cleanup cannot obtain terminal
evidence, the cancellation boundary becomes =indeterminate=.

* Coalesced demand accounting

Multiple joined demands sharing one provider operation consume one effect
attempt permit, not one permit per waiter.

Each joined demand retains separate boundary-scoped authority and budget
attribution.  Withdrawing one demand:

- removes only that demand's authority and attribution;
- does not return the attempt permit while another authorized waiter remains;
- does not cancel the provider attempt implicitly.

The attempt permit returns only after terminal operation evidence and any
required provider cleanup are recorded.

* Backpressure condition

Capacity unavailability suspends the demand through a structured continuable
backpressure condition.  It is not:

- a dependency cycle;
- an effect indeterminacy barrier;
- proof of host resource exhaustion;
- an ordinary provider failure;
- authority for immediate retry.

Policy-visible recovery may:

- defer until a capacity receipt becomes available;
- refine the boundary budget within authority;
- select another already qualified provider capacity domain;
- reduce or withdraw ordinary work;
- request boundary cancellation;
- escalate to the containing boundary.

There is no unconditional =retry= restart.  Wake-up requires new capacity,
budget, provider, or policy evidence.

Suspension uses continuation/event notification supplied by the runtime.  POO
Flow MUST NOT implement busy polling.

* Starvation and progress evidence

POO Flow does not prescribe a scheduler algorithm, but it requires evidence
that an admitted or backpressured demand is making, awaiting, or denied
progress.

Starvation policy may use:

- ordered admission and return receipts;
- provider queue-position evidence when available;
- boundary progress budgets;
- competing admission-class evidence;
- cleanup-reserve availability;
- causal wait-graph edges.

Elapsed time alone does not prove starvation or capacity exhaustion.

A policy threshold produces a structured starvation-escalation condition.  It
does not reorder POO lineage, steal unrelated boundary authority, or execute
work directly.

* Precedence and execution concurrency

POO prototype refinement precedence and runtime execution order are different
dimensions:

#+begin_src mermaid
flowchart LR
  L["State: boundary-local ordered POO lineage"] -->|"semantic normalization"| V["State: deterministic prototype value"]
  S1["State: sibling demand A"] -->|"independent capacity permit"| E1["State: concurrent execution A"]
  S2["State: sibling demand B"] -->|"independent capacity permit"| E2["State: concurrent execution B"]
  E1 -->|"receipt-qualified value"| V
  E2 -->|"receipt-qualified value"| V
  E1 -.->|"execution timing cannot change refinement precedence"| L
  E2 -.->|"execution timing cannot change refinement precedence"| L
#+end_src

Sibling structural boundaries may execute concurrently when dependency,
provider, policy, and capacity evidence allow.  Concurrency MUST NOT flatten
identity boundaries, introduce cross-root =super=, or alter boundary-local
refinement order.

* Wait-graph projection

Admission adds typed edges and nodes to the existing wait graph:

- demand-to-budget authority;
- demand-to-provider capacity;
- backpressure suspension;
- capacity return;
- cleanup reserve;
- parent/child budget allocation;
- starvation escalation.

Graph inspection is read-only.  It does not issue permits, return capacity,
resume continuations, or schedule work.

A closed graph with no progress authority may produce structured deadlock,
starvation, capacity-unobservable, or cancellation-indeterminacy evidence.
These diagnoses remain distinct.

* Observability and receipt contract

The admission receipt chain records:

- realization context, boundary, and generation;
- admission class;
- demand or coalesced attempt identity;
- budget allocation and containment path;
- provider capacity domain and permit evidence;
- admission, defer, wake-up, return, transfer, and revocation decisions;
- backpressure or starvation escalation;
- causal predecessor receipt.

Observability reports definition, realization, and governance projections
without exposing runtime queue mutation or a transferable permit.

No sink may admit work, change budget allocation, return capacity, cancel a
boundary, or resume a suspended continuation.

Replay verifies historical admission and capacity receipts according to
[[file:45-14-deterministic-realization-replay.org][Deterministic Realization Replay]].  It never recreates a capacity permit or
attempts to reproduce wall-clock queue timing.

Admission and capacity receipts use the identity, projection, predecessor, and
diagnostic-annotation semantics defined by
[[file:45-15-receipt-object-canonical-evidence-projection.org][Receipt Object And Canonical Evidence Projection]].

* API evidence gate

The semantics in this module are normative.  Exact admission, permit, budget,
backpressure, and starvation identifiers remain =semantic-required= until the
relevant =gerbil-poo= and runtime-provider surface is symbol-located,
behavior-proven, and flow-qualified.

Any generally reusable POO demand-admission or suspension mechanism belongs
upstream in =gerbil-poo=.  Runtime scheduling remains a provider concern.  POO
Flow MUST NOT ship a shadow scheduler, queue runtime, fixed-point evaluator, or
host-capacity guesser.

* Explicit exclusions

This RFC does not authorize:

- a POO Flow private scheduler;
- eager admission caused by graph traversal;
- busy polling under backpressure;
- capacity permits serialized across contexts;
- budget ownership transfer through Mix imports;
- one effect permit per joined waiter on a coalesced attempt;
- ordinary work consuming the final cleanup authority without revocation proof;
- new ordinary admission after cancellation;
- capacity uncertainty treated as permission to overcommit;
- execution timing changing POO refinement precedence;
- a public scheduling DSL before the real API is behavior-proven.
