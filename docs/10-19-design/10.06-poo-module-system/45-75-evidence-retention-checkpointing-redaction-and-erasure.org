#+title: Evidence Retention, Checkpointing, Redaction, and Erasure
#+startup: overview

* Status

This RFC records the accepted retention, compaction, disclosure, and physical
erasure semantics for POO Flow evidence.

It extends immutable receipts, truth maintenance, governance, continuations,
reconciliation, and materialization reclamation.

* Typst formalization: immutable history and reclaimable evidence bodies

Logical evidence history remains immutable, but this does not require permanent
retention of every raw payload, index, encryption key, or physical replica.
Erasure admission requires the complete retention and governance boundary.

#+begin_src typst
#let erasure-checks(evidence, current) = (
  retention-window-expired(evidence, current.time-observation)
  and not legal-hold-active(evidence)
  and checkpoint-coverage-established(evidence)
  and replay-requirements-preserved(evidence)
  and reference-closure-established(evidence)
  and valid-one-shot-governance-capability(evidence)
  and replica-plan-complete(evidence)
)

$ retention-outcome(evidence)
  ==> not rewrite(logical-history(evidence)) $

$ erase(evidence)
  ==> erasure-checks(evidence, current)
  and preserve(canonical-receipt-header(evidence)) $

$ legal-hold-active(evidence) ==> not erase(evidence) $
#+end_src

Redaction is a disclosure projection and never claims physical erasure.
Checkpoint compaction preserves replay coverage.  Physical or cryptographic
erasure requires receipts covering key destruction, every required replica,
payload unavailability, and preservation of the canonical header.

#+begin_src typst
$ redact(evidence, projection)
  ==> commitment(projection) = commitment(evidence)
  and not claims-physical-erasure(projection) $

$ compact(checkpoint, evidence-range)
  ==> covers(checkpoint, evidence-range)
  and preserves(checkpoint.replay-contract) $

$ erasure-receipt(receipt)
  ==> key-destroyed(receipt.key)
  and forall(
    replica in receipt.required-replicas,
    replica in receipt.purged-replicas,
  )
  and payload-unavailable(receipt.evidence)
  and header-preserved(receipt.evidence) $

$ policy-a != policy-b or observation-a != observation-b
  ==> retention-decision-id(evidence, policy-a, observation-a)
  != retention-decision-id(evidence, policy-b, observation-b) $
#+end_src

* Central decision

Logical evidence history is not rewritten.

Evidence payloads, sensitive materializations, indexes, encryption keys, and
physical replicas do not need to remain resident forever when an admitted
retention policy permits their reclamation.

Immutable audit history does not mean permanent retention of all raw sensitive
data.

* POO semantic ownership

Evidence retention policy is represented by ordinary higher-order POO objects.
Native prototype composition defines:

- evidence classification;
- retention windows;
- replay requirements;
- checkpoint coverage;
- disclosure projections;
- redaction;
- destructive erasure;
- legal holds;
- reference closure;
- reclamation receipts.

The public extension interface remains POO-native. Retention, deletion,
redaction, and replay level must not become unstructured macro tokens or raw
storage callbacks.

Pure policy may produce proposals. It has no authority to delete a payload,
destroy a key, purge a replica, or release a legal hold.

* Evidence layers

Evidence is separated into at least four layers.

** Canonical receipt header

The canonical header records:

- evidence identity;
- schema identity;
- producer or authority;
- causal parents;
- semantic cut;
- action or result role;
- content commitment;
- retention classification;
- disclosure classification.

It is the minimum logical history skeleton.

** Evidence body

The evidence body contains the complete observation, proof, trace, decision, or
resource receipt required for independent verification or replay.

Retention policy states whether and for how long the complete body is required.

** Sensitive payload

Sensitive payload may contain:

- secrets;
- personal data;
- raw entropy;
- credential material;
- restricted business evidence;
- confidential source material.

It is independently encrypted and disclosure-scoped. Public access to the
canonical header does not grant access to the sensitive payload.

** Operational indexes

Operational indexes include:

- reverse-dependency indexes;
- search indexes;
- caches;
- materialized explanation views;
- query projections.

An index is not canonical history. It may be rebuilt when the required source
evidence remains available.

* Retention policy input

A pure retention policy evaluates:

- evidence role;
- causal dependents;
- active generation, continuation, effect, and reconciliation references;
- governance and audit obligations;
- legal hold;
- jurisdiction and purpose;
- confidentiality domain;
- required replay level;
- retention window;
- deletion authority requirements.

Window expiry produces a proposal. It does not perform reclamation.

* Evidence checkpoint

An immutable =EvidenceCheckpoint= records:

- covered evidence identities or ranges;
- causal closure boundary;
- canonical root commitment;
- included schema identities;
- excluded, missing, or unavailable evidence;
- coverage policy;
- checkpoint producer;
- verification algorithm identity;
- confidentiality domain;
- checkpoint receipt identity.

The checkpoint is new evidence. It does not modify or retroactively replace the
original receipts.

A root commitment may prove integrity and inclusion for committed content. It
does not prove semantic correctness and does not make a deleted payload
available.

* Replay levels

Every checkpoint declares the replay capability that remains after retention
actions.

** Full replay

Original evidence bodies and all required deterministic inputs remain
available.

** Proof-verifiable replay

Enough witnesses remain to independently verify the declared proof set, but
some unrelated raw evidence may be absent.

** Audit-summary verification

The checkpoint proves a historical summary and commitments, but does not
support full semantic reevaluation.

** Identity-only retention

Only the minimum evidence that an action, decision, or receipt existed remains.

An audit summary or identity-only record must not be advertised as full replay.

* Disclosure redaction

Disclosure redaction derives a restricted projection while retaining the
original evidence in a higher-authority domain.

The projection has:

- its own identity;
- source evidence identity;
- redaction policy identity;
- disclosed and withheld field roles;
- derivation receipt;
- confidentiality domain.

Redaction does not change the original evidence identity or bytes.

* Destructive erasure

Destructive erasure removes a payload, encryption key, materialization, replica,
or backup.

It requires:

- exact target identity;
- legal and policy authority;
- store, replica, and backup scope;
- encryption-key scope;
- destruction method;
- current retention and legal-hold observations;
- verification evidence;
- partial and unknown outcome policy;
- erasure receipt identity.

Deleting a payload does not delete the historical fact that an erasure action
occurred.

* Cryptographic erasure

Destroying an encryption key proves effective erasure only for the scope whose
recoverability depended exclusively on that key.

The evidence closure covers:

- key copies;
- plaintext replicas;
- derived caches;
- backups;
- exported material;
- key-management result;
- pending recovery or replication operations.

When this closure is incomplete, the result is partial or unknown erasure.

Key destruction must not be described as complete erasure merely because the
primary key-management request succeeded.

* Legal hold and retention override

Legal hold and other explicit preservation obligations override ordinary
retention-window expiry.

Potential blockers include:

- open incident;
- unresolved effect;
- pending reconciliation;
- governance review;
- suspended continuation;
- litigation or regulatory hold;
- active audit obligation;
- uncompensatable-effect review.

The blocker has an exact identity, scope, authority, and release condition.

Release of a hold is a new authoritative observation. It does not directly
delete evidence.

* Reference closure

Evidence bodies must remain sufficiently available while depended upon by:

- active proof;
- current conclusion;
- issued but unconsumed capability;
- suspended continuation;
- pending effect or reconciliation;
- open reversion window;
- uncompensatable-effect review;
- active governance decision.

Retention policy records the verification level required by each dependent.

If reclamation removes a witness needed for independent verification, the
dependent proof is marked as no longer independently verifiable. It must not
continue to cross an admission boundary as a complete proof.

A digest alone does not replace a required witness.

* Schema evolution

Old evidence is not rewritten into a new schema.

Schema evolution retains:

- original schema identity;
- original canonical bytes or commitment;
- original verifier identity;
- migration or projection receipt;
- new derived view identity.

A new schema view is a derived projection. It must not impersonate the original
receipt format.

Verifier retirement must preserve the verification evidence promised by the
declared replay level.

* Confidential commitments

Merkle leaves, paths, digests, and membership queries may disclose sensitive
information.

Policy may use:

- confidentiality-domain-specific trees;
- salted or keyed commitments;
- restricted inclusion proofs;
- separate public and private checkpoints;
- disclosure-aware verification receipts.

A public root commitment does not authorize unrestricted membership queries.

* Evidence reclamation receipt

Every reclamation or erasure attempt emits an
=EvidenceReclamationReceipt= recording:

- targets removed;
- evidence retained;
- policy and authority;
- covered stores, replicas, and backups;
- key-destruction result;
- residual commitments;
- success, failure, or unknown outcome per target;
- remaining replay and verification level;
- reconciliation requirement.

A single global deletion-success flag must not hide per-resource outcomes.

* Failure taxonomy

The implementation must distinguish at least:

- checkpoint coverage incomplete;
- causal closure incomplete;
- root commitment verification failed;
- replay level overstated;
- redaction projection invalid;
- sensitive payload disclosure denied;
- retention reference still active;
- legal hold active;
- destructive authority missing;
- key copy unresolved;
- plaintext replica unresolved;
- backup erasure unknown;
- dependent proof no longer independently verifiable;
- schema verifier unavailable;
- confidential commitment disclosure violation;
- erasure reconciliation incomplete.

* Required invariants

1. Logical evidence history is not rewritten.
2. Receipt header, body, sensitive payload, and operational index remain
   separate.
3. Retention-window expiry produces a proposal, not deletion.
4. Checkpoints are new evidence and declare exact coverage.
5. Commitments do not prove semantic correctness or payload availability.
6. Replay capability is declared without exaggeration.
7. Disclosure redaction does not modify original evidence.
8. Destructive erasure requires exact authority and receipts.
9. Cryptographic erasure covers keys, plaintext, replicas, caches, and backups.
10. Legal holds override ordinary retention expiry.
11. Reference closure preserves required verification witnesses.
12. Missing witnesses downgrade proof usability explicitly.
13. Schema evolution derives new views without rewriting old receipts.
14. Confidential commitments remain disclosure-scoped.
15. Partial and unknown erasure outcomes remain visible.
16. Public retention policy extension remains POO-native.
